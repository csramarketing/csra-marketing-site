Chapter 01

WordPress is free. Your WordPress site is not.

The WordPress software itself costs nothing. That is where the free part ends.

A real roofing website on WordPress still needs hosting, a premium theme, a page builder license, a form plugin, a backup plugin, a security plugin, and someone to keep all of it updated. Those costs are small individually. Together they add up to a monthly bill most roofers never budgeted for.

Here is a typical market range for what a WordPress roofing site actually costs to run: hosting $20 to $50 a month, premium theme around $60 one time, plugin licenses $100 to $300 a year, and maintenance either your own Sunday afternoons or $100 to $300 a month to someone else. None of that is unreasonable. It just is not free.

A custom site flips the math. You pay more upfront and almost nothing after. No plugin licenses. No page builder renewals. Hosting and a domain, and you are done.

Neither option is wrong. But go in knowing the real numbers, not the sticker price. If you want the full picture of what roofing websites cost across the board, I broke it down here. Chapter 02

WordPress powers 40% of the web. That makes it a target.

According to W3Techs' ongoing crawl data, WordPress runs on roughly 41% of all websites as of mid-2026, down a few points from its peak near 43%. It is still by far the most popular way to build a website on earth.

Here is the deal: that popularity is also its biggest security problem. When two out of five websites run the same software, hackers learn that software inside and out. Every known weakness gets probed millions of times a day.

Now, WordPress core itself is well maintained and reasonably secure. The weak point is almost never WordPress. It is the plugins. Patchstack's State of WordPress Security 2026 report found that 91% of the 11,334 new WordPress vulnerabilities disclosed in 2025 sat in plugins. WordPress core accounted for just 6.

In my experience reviewing roofing sites, a typical WordPress install runs 15 to 25 active plugins. That is 15 to 25 separate doors into your website, each maintained by a different developer with different standards.

Why?

Because every plugin author updates on their own schedule, and the week a popular plugin has a known hole is the week every unpatched site using it gets probed. Your roof replacement page does not need to be interesting to a hacker. It just needs to be running the vulnerable plugin.

A custom site has no plugins. There is simply less to attack. That does not make it invincible, but it removes the most common way roofing websites get hacked.

Chapter 03

Every plugin you add makes your site a little slower

Speed is where this debate gets decided for most roofers, because speed is where jobs get won or lost.

Every plugin on a WordPress site loads its own code on every page. Your contact form plugin, your slider, your review widget, your chat popup, your cookie banner. Each one adds weight. And most roofing sites are built on heavy multipurpose themes that load features you will never use: animation libraries, icon packs, demo layouts for businesses you do not run.

Google's own research has shown that bounce probability climbs steeply as pages get slower. A homeowner with a leaking roof is not waiting around. They tap back and call the next roofer.

Here is what that means in plain terms: a slow site does not just annoy people, it hands your jobs to competitors. The homeowner never sees your reviews, your workmanship, or your pricing. They just see a blank screen and leave.

A custom site starts lean and stays lean. There is no page builder injecting extra code, no plugin stack fighting for load order. The site contains exactly what it needs and nothing it does not.

Can a WordPress site be fast? Yes. It takes real optimization work: caching, image compression, script cleanup, a lightweight theme. Plenty of fast WordPress sites exist. But speed on WordPress is something you have to fight for continuously. On a custom site, it is the default.

Chapter 04

A custom site loads fast and stays fast

Let me put the custom side of the case plainly, because this is where it wins.

A custom-built site is just the code your pages need. No database queries for features you do not use. No plugin updates that suddenly slow everything down. No page builder wrapping your content in layers of markup. The site you launch on day one is the site you have on day 400.

That consistency matters more than most roofers realize. WordPress sites tend to get slower over time as plugins pile up and updates stack. A custom site does not degrade the same way because there is nothing piling up.

There is a trade-off, and I will not hide it: when you want to change something on a custom site, you usually need the person who built it. Adding a new service page or swapping your headline is not a five-minute DIY job the way it can be in WordPress. You are trading independence for performance.

For most roofing companies, that is a good trade. You are not publishing blog posts every week. You need a site that loads instantly, looks professional, and turns visitors into calls. A custom site does exactly that, every day, without asking anything of you.

Chapter 05

Best website platform for roofers: the honest cost comparison

Let us put real numbers next to each other. These are typical market ranges, not quotes, and your mileage will vary.

WordPress route, first year (worked example):
Hosting: $300 to $600
Premium theme: ~$60
Plugin licenses: $100 to $300
Professional setup and design: $1,500 to $5,000 (typical market range)
Maintenance (your time or someone else's): $1,200 to $3,600
First-year realistic total: $3,000 to $9,500

Custom site, first year (worked example):
Design and build: $2,500 to $10,000 (typical market range)
Hosting and domain: $150 to $300
Maintenance: near zero
First-year realistic total: $2,650 to $10,300

Notice how close those ranges are. WordPress looks cheaper until you count the ongoing costs. A custom site looks expensive until you realize the spending stops.

The real question when you pick the best website platform for roofers is not which costs less in year one. It is which one is still fast, secure, and working hard for you in year three without you thinking about it.

Chapter 06

Who fixes it when something breaks at 9 PM

This is the chapter nobody writes about, and it is the one that decides the most outcomes.

On WordPress, something will eventually break. A plugin updates and conflicts with your theme. Your contact form stops sending emails. An update fails halfway and takes the site down. When that happens on a Tuesday night, the fix is on you or on whoever you pay for maintenance.

If you are handy and do not mind it, fine. But most roofers did not get into business to troubleshoot plugin conflicts. Every hour you spend fixing your website is an hour you are not estimating jobs.

On a custom site, there is far less to break. No plugin updates means no update conflicts. The site either works or it does not, and when it does not, it is one phone call to the person who built it.

Be honest with yourself about which kind of owner you are. If you like tinkering and want control, WordPress gives you that. If you want a site you never think about, custom is the calmer life.

Chapter 07

The verdict: buy the outcome, not the platform

Here is my honest take after watching roofing companies wrestle with this for years.

Choosing the best website platform for roofers matters far less than the outcome you get from it. What you actually need is a site that loads in under two seconds, looks like a company you would trust on your roof, shows your reviews, and makes it dead simple to call you. WordPress can do that. A custom site can do that. The logo in the footer does not book jobs.

So decide on practical grounds:

Pick WordPress if you want to make your own edits, you (or someone you trust) will stay on top of updates, and you are comfortable with ongoing maintenance costs.

Pick custom if you want maximum speed, minimum maintenance, and you are fine calling your builder when you need changes.

Frequently asked questions

Is WordPress bad for roofing companies?

No. WordPress is a legitimate platform that powers some of the biggest sites on the web. For a roofing company it works fine as long as someone stays on top of updates, security, and speed optimization. The problems start when a WordPress site gets built and then ignored for two years.

Can a WordPress site load as fast as a custom site?

Yes, but it takes deliberate ongoing work: caching, image optimization, a lightweight theme, and regular plugin audits. A custom site is fast by default because there is no plugin bloat to fight. On WordPress, speed is a maintenance task. On custom, it is a design property.

How much does a custom website cost for a roofer?

Typical market range is $2,500 to $10,000 for the build, depending on page count and design complexity, plus minimal ongoing costs. WordPress often lands between $3,000 and $9,500 in the first year once you count hosting, plugins, setup, and maintenance. Full cost breakdown here.

Is WordPress secure enough for a roofing website?

WordPress core is secure enough. The risk comes from plugins: 91% of new WordPress vulnerabilities in 2025 traced back to third-party plugins, not core (per Patchstack's 2026 security report). Keep plugins minimal, updated, and from reputable developers, and the risk drops sharply. A custom site sidesteps this entirely by having no plugins.

Do I have to update my WordPress site myself?

Someone has to. WordPress core, themes, and plugins release updates regularly, and skipping them is how sites get hacked or break. You can do it yourself if you are comfortable, or pay $100 to $300 a month (typical market range) for a maintenance plan. A custom site has essentially nothing to update.

Will Google rank a custom site better than WordPress?

No. Google ranks pages, not platforms. What Google cares about is speed, mobile experience, content quality, and relevance. A fast WordPress site will outrank a slow custom site every time, and vice versa. Pick the platform you will actually maintain, then make it fast.

Can I switch from WordPress to a custom site later?

Yes. It is a rebuild, not a migration: the new site gets built, content moves over, URLs get redirected so you do not lose rankings. Most rebuilds take a few weeks. If your WordPress site is slow, insecure, or embarrassing, rebuilding on a lean custom stack is often cheaper than trying to fix years of plugin debt.
