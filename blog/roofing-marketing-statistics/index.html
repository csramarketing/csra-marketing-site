Section 01 · Stats 1 to 5

How Homeowners Search: Almost Every Job Starts Online

1.84% of consumers searched online for a local business in the last three months

According to BrightLocal's Consumer Search Behavior 2026 study, nearly every homeowner with a roof problem starts online. If you are not visible there, you do not exist to them.

2.52% start their local search on Google Search, and another 9% start on Google Maps

BrightLocal's channel research found that six in ten local searches start on a Google property. That is where the roofing customer journey begins, long before your phone rings. Your Google presence is your front door.

3.72% of consumers look at three or fewer businesses before deciding

The same BrightLocal research shows homeowners have a short list, and it is very short. You are not competing with every roofer in town. You are competing to be one of the three a homeowner actually considers. Only 1% look at more than ten businesses.

4.45% used AI tools for local business recommendations in the last year, up from 6% the year before

This one surprised me. BrightLocal found AI recommendations are the fastest-growing discovery channel in local search, and 31% of consumers now use them at least monthly. AI tools pull from your reviews, your profile and your website, so the basics matter more than ever.

5.Half of smartphone users who run a local search visit a business within a day

Google's local advertising research puts hard numbers on intent: a local search is not casual browsing. On phones, half of those searchers are at someone's door within 24 hours. On computer and tablet, the figure is 34%, which is still fast.

Section 02 · Stats 6 to 10

Google Business Profile and Maps: Three Spots Take a Third of the Clicks

6.Your primary Google Business Profile category is the #1 ranking factor for the map pack

Whitespark's Local Search Ranking Factors 2026, which scored 187 factors with 47 local search experts, puts your primary category at the top. Picking "Roofing contractor" as your primary category is the single highest-leverage setting in your profile. It takes two minutes to check.

Why does this matter so much?

Because the map pack is where the clicks are, and we will get to that number in a second.

7.Seven of the ten highest-ranked map pack factors are fields inside your Google Business Profile

Primary category, keywords in the business title, address in the city of search, being open at the time of search, address showing, additional categories and proper map pin placement. Per Whitespark, most of what moves the map pack is free to fix. It is profile housekeeping, not ad spend. I walk through the profile side of this in my guide to local SEO for roofers.

8.Customers are 2.7 times more likely to consider a business reputable with a complete Business Profile

That figure comes from research Google commissioned on its own profiles, so weigh it accordingly, but the direction is hard to argue with. A half-finished profile does not just rank lower, it makes you look less trustworthy before a homeowner ever reaches your website. The empty fields you have been ignoring are quietly costing you credibility.

9.The average local business is found in 1,009 Google searches a month, and 84% are discovery searches

BrightLocal's study of 45,000 listings found the typical business shows up in about a thousand searches monthly. Here is the kicker: 84% of those searches come from people searching a service, not a business name. Homeowners find roofers by searching "roof repair near me," not by typing your company name.

10.The map pack receives 32% of all clicks on a local results page

On Google results pages without Local Services Ads, BrightLocal's click research found the three map listings take roughly a third of every click. Being outside the pack means fighting over what is left. Three spots, a third of the demand.

Key takeaway (worked example, illustrative numbers): Say your market produces 300 searches a month for roofers. A third of the clicks, roughly 96, land on the three map pack listings. If being in that pack instead of under it wins you just one extra booked job a month, at a $12,000 average roof and a 40% gross margin, that is $4,800 in gross profit a month, or $57,600 a year. That is what "ranking higher" actually means in dollars.

Section 03 · Stats 11 to 15

Reviews: The Decision Happens Before Your Phone Rings

11.Only 4% of consumers never look at reviews before choosing a local business

Flip it around: 96% of your future customers read reviews, according to BrightLocal's Local Consumer Review Survey 2025. Reviews are not a nice-to-have section of your marketing. For most homeowners, they are the decision.

12.42% of consumers trust online reviews as much as a personal recommendation

BrightLocal's review survey found strangers' reviews now carry close to the same weight as a neighbor's referral. Word of mouth still matters, but your review profile is word of mouth at scale, visible to every homeowner who searches your name.

13.20% of consumers say reviews from the last two weeks are the ones that impact their decision

Recency is part of the sale. BrightLocal found one in five consumers weighs the freshest reviews most. A pile of five-star reviews from 2022 does not close jobs in 2027. You need a steady drip of new ones.

14.Reviews are three of the top local pack ranking factors

High numerical Google ratings rank #6, quantity of Google reviews with text ranks #9, and recency of reviews ranks #11 in Whitespark's 2026 factor rankings. Rating, volume and freshness all move the map pack. One without the others leaves rankings on the table.

15.48% of consumers read Google's AI review summary first, and 18% would decide from that summary alone

BrightLocal's survey found nearly half of consumers start with the AI summary Google writes about your reviews. One in five may never read a single full review. The themes across your reviews, good and bad, now write your first impression.

Websites and Mobile: Your Site Is a Phone Experience First

16.73% of local searches start on a mobile device

BrightLocal's channel data breaks local search down by device: 73% mobile, 19% computer, 8% tablet. Your website is a phone experience first. If it is clunky on a phone, it fails three out of four homeowners before they see a word of your pitch.

Where local searches start, by device

Source: BrightLocal, Consumer Search Behavior 2026.

17.53% of mobile site visits are abandoned if the page takes longer than three seconds to load

Google's mobile performance research is blunt: cross the three-second line and you throw away half the homeowners your marketing already paid to attract. Speed is not a tech detail. It is a lead leak.

18.The #1 organic result on Google gets an average click-through rate of 27.6%

Backlinko's analysis of 4 million search results shows the top spot takes more than a quarter of all clicks. The top position in your city is not a vanity metric. It is the single biggest slice of demand on the page. This is a big part of why I say roofing SEO works when it is given time to compound.

19.Only 0.63% of Google searchers click anything on the second page of results

Same Backlinko study: page two is not "almost there." For practical purposes, page two is invisible. If your site sits there for your money keywords, homeowners will never know you exist.

20.96.55% of all web pages get zero search traffic from Google

Ahrefs analyzed roughly 14 billion pages and found almost all of them get nothing. Publishing pages is not a strategy. It is why most roofer websites sit idle while a handful of well-built competitors take the calls. Section 05 · Stats 21 to 23

Leads and Advertising Costs: A $103.6 Billion Market, 113,000 Competitors

Here's the deal: attention is only worth chasing because the prize behind it is huge. These three numbers size up the fight.

21.The US roofing contractors industry is a $103.6 billion market in 2026

IBISWorld's 2026 industry report sizes the prize, and it is enormous. The fight is not over whether the work exists. It is over who shows up when a homeowner in your zip code searches.

22.There are 113,000 roofing businesses in the US, growing at 4.2% per year

IBISWorld counts the competition, and it grows every year. Standing still on marketing means falling behind by default, because the new company down the road is not standing still.

23.Four in five consumers want ads customized to their city, zip code or immediate surroundings

Google's local advertising research explains why generic roofing ads underperform: homeowners respond to ads that name their area. If you run ads, local detail is not decoration. Section 06 · Stats 24 to 27

Follow-Up and Sales: The Fastest Roofer Usually Wins

24.75% of consumers choose a local business in under 30 minutes, and 28% decide in under five minutes

BrightLocal's decision speed research should change how you run your office. The homeowner who finds you at 9:00 has picked a roofer by 9:30, often by 9:05. Slow response is the same as no response.

25.Only 7% of consumers do not expect a response to their review

BrightLocal's review survey found 63% expect a response within two to three days to a week. An unanswered review reads as indifference to every homeowner who sees it afterwards, not just the person who wrote it. Answering reviews is marketing, not admin.

26.Email is the review request channel customers respond to most, at 40%

Asked how they are most likely to write a review, BrightLocal's respondents picked email at 40%, ahead of an in-person ask at 27%. The ask matters more than the hope. A simple post-job email beats waiting for happy customers to think of it.

27.96% of consumers are open to writing a review when asked

Last one, and it might be the most useful number in this whole post. BrightLocal found the reviews are there for the asking. Most roofers simply never build the ask into the job process. Build it in, and stats 11 through 15 start working for you instead of against you.

The takeaway

The Bottom Line: One System, Not Three Projects

Every one of these 27 numbers points the same direction. Homeowners search on Google, shortlist three companies, read the freshest reviews, and hire whoever responds first. Your Google profile, your reviews and your website are not three separate projects. They are one system, and the owners who treat them that way take the jobs.

Roofing marketing statistics questions, answered

How much should a roofing company spend on marketing?

A common planning rule is 5% to 10% of gross revenue, with newer companies pushing toward the top of that range while they build a name. Treat that as planning guidance, not a measured industry average, because no original study pins down one number for roofers. What matters more is where it goes: the stats above show homeowners deciding on Google, on reviews and on your website, so that is where the budget earns its keep.

How do homeowners actually find a roofing company in 2027?

Mostly on Google. 84% of consumers searched online for a local business in the last three months (BrightLocal), 52% started their most recent local search on Google Search, and 45% used AI tools for local recommendations in the last year. Referrals still happen, but even referred homeowners usually check your profile and reviews before they call.

Do Google reviews really bring in roofing jobs, or are they just nice to have?

They bring in jobs. Only 4% of consumers never look at reviews (BrightLocal), 42% trust them as much as a personal recommendation, and ratings, review volume and recency are three of the top local pack ranking factors in Whitespark's 2026 rankings. Reviews help you rank, and then they help you close.

How fast do homeowners choose a roofer after they start searching?

Fast. 75% choose a local business in under 30 minutes, and 28% decide in under five minutes (BrightLocal). Half of smartphone users who run a local search visit a business within a day (Google). Speed of response is a real competitive edge.

How many roofing companies am I competing against?

IBISWorld counts 113,000 roofing businesses in the US in 2026, growing at 4.2% per year. Locally, the fight is narrower: 72% of consumers look at three or fewer businesses before deciding (BrightLocal), so your real competition is whoever takes those three spots in your market.

Is SEO or Google Ads better for getting roofing leads?

They do different jobs. Ads buy visibility that stops the day you stop paying, and four in five consumers want those ads customized to their city or zip (Google). SEO and your Google Business Profile build an asset: the map pack takes 32% of clicks on a local results page (BrightLocal) without a per-click cost. Most growing roofers run both: ads for now, SEO for keeps.

How long does it take for a roofing website to start getting traffic from Google?

There is no honest fixed number, and anyone who gives you one is guessing. What the data does show is that 96.55% of web pages get zero search traffic (Ahrefs), so publishing alone changes nothing. Traffic follows rankings, reviews and links built over months, which is why the owners who win treat it as a system, not a launch.

What happens if my roofing website is slow on phones?

You lose the majority of your visitors. 53% of mobile site visits are abandoned if a page takes longer than three seconds to load (Google), and 73% of local searches start on a mobile device (BrightLocal). A slow phone experience quietly cancels out your ads, your rankings and your reviews.
