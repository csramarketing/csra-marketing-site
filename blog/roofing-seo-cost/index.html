Chapter 01

The short answer

Most roofing companies pay $1,500 to $5,000 a month for SEO.

Where you land depends mostly on your market.

Here's how the range breaks down:

  • Under $500/month: automated reports and directory listings. Rankings rarely move.
  • $1,000 to $2,000/month: a real local campaign. Google Business Profile work, reviews, citations and a few useful pages a month. Fits small and mid-size markets.
  • $2,000 to $5,000/month: a competitive campaign. Everything above, plus steady content, authority links and technical work. Built for metros where other roofers are paying for SEO too.
  • $5,000+/month: multi-location companies and aggressive growth plays.

Here's the thing:

The monthly fee is the wrong number to stare at.

The right number is what one roof is worth to you. A replacement typically runs $8,000 to $15,000 (illustrative: swap in your own average job size).

Chapter 02

The 5 things that push your price up or down

Two roofers can get SEO quotes $3,000 a month apart for what sounds like the same service.

Here's why.

1. Your competition

This is the big one.

Ranking in a town with 6 roofers costs a fraction of ranking in a metro with 60. Every competitor paying for SEO raises the bar for you.

A good provider prices against your actual competition. Not a national average.

2. Your starting point

A brand-new site with 12 reviews needs months of foundation work.

A 5-year-old site with 200 reviews needs maintenance and a push.

Starting from zero costs more. There's simply more to build.

3. How many towns you want

One city is the baseline.

Every extra town adds work: another location page, another map pack to fight for, more reviews to earn. Expect the price to grow with your service area.

4. What's actually included

"SEO" isn't one thing.

An $800 package might be listings and a monthly report. A $3,000 package might include content, links, technical fixes, a review system and Google Business Profile management.

Bottom line? Compare scopes, not prices.

5. Who does the work

A solo freelancer has low overhead and can charge less. An agency costs more but should bring a team and accountability.

Either can work. Just know what you're paying for.

Pro tip: Ask every provider to quote against the same 3 to 5 competitors in your town. It's the fastest way to compare apples to apples.

Expect automated listings, a monthly PDF and very little human strategy.

Content is thin or templated. Links are missing or low quality.

  • Google Business Profile optimization and ongoing management
  • A system for earning reviews
  • Citation cleanup and building
  • A few genuinely useful pages a month

In small and mid-size markets, this tier can start winning map pack spots within 4 to 8 months.

This tier is for crowded metros and roofers who want to own a market, not just show up in it.

Chapter 04

Why SEO is monthly (not a one-time fix)

Why pay every month when a website is a one-time build? Here's the answer in 3 parts.

1. Google never sits still. Updates roll out all year. Competitors publish new pages and earn new reviews every week. A one-time fix fades because everything around it keeps moving.

2. Authority stacks. Every month of good content, real reviews and solid links builds on the last. Month 6 beats month 2 because the foundation is bigger. That's also why SEO gets cheaper per lead over time.

3. The work never ends. Review requests go out weekly. New pages get published. Technical issues get caught and fixed. None of that happens once.

So be careful with anyone selling "one-time SEO."

Usually that means a one-time audit with a few fixes. Useful? Sure. A growth plan? No.

It's a tune-up, not an engine.

Chapter 06

Red flags: how roofers get burned

Some SEO providers take monthly payments and deliver monthly PDFs. Here's how to spot them.

  • Guaranteed rankings. Nobody can guarantee the top spot on Google. A guarantee is a confession.
  • "SEO" for $99 to $299 a month. At that price, no human is doing real work on your account.
  • You don't own your accounts. If they won't give you admin access to your Google Business Profile, website or analytics, they're building leverage over you. Not rankings for you.
  • Vague monthly plans. You should get a plain-English answer to "What will you do this month?" If you don't, the work probably isn't happening.
  • Long contracts with no exit. A 12-month lock-in with no performance clause protects the provider. Not you.
  • Reports with no revenue talk. If month 6 arrives and you're still talking keyword positions instead of calls and jobs, something's off.

Chapter 07

7 questions to ask before you pay

Use these on every sales call. Good providers will welcome them.

  • What exactly will you do in the first 90 days? You want a plan, not "we'll optimize."
  • How do you report results? Are they tied to calls and revenue?
  • Who owns the content, links and accounts if we part ways?
  • What happens to my rankings if I cancel?
  • Can you show me a sample monthly report?
  • How do you earn reviews without breaking Google's rules?
  • What's the contract term, and what's the exit clause?

Here's my rule: if a provider stumbles on more than one of these, keep looking.

FAQ

Roofing SEO cost questions, answered

How much does SEO cost for a roofing company?

Most roofing companies pay $1,500 to $5,000 a month for SEO that moves rankings. Smaller markets can win at the low end. Crowded metros need the high end. Under $500 a month rarely moves anything.

Can I pay for SEO once instead of monthly?

A one-time audit and fix-up has value, but it won't keep growing your rankings. Google and your competitors keep changing, so SEO needs ongoing content, reviews, links and technical upkeep. Budget monthly for at least 6 months.

How long until roofing SEO pays off?

Plan on 4 to 8 months as a working guideline before a campaign hits its stride. The first months build the foundation. Roofers who quit at month 3 usually never see the return.

Is $500 a month SEO worth it for a roofer?

Usually not. At $500 a month there's no budget for real strategy, quality content or legitimate links. You get automated reports and directory listings.

What is actually included in roofing SEO?

A complete campaign covers Google Business Profile management, review generation, citations, technical website fixes, content and authority links. If a proposal doesn't list monthly deliverables, ask what your money buys.

Should I hire an SEO company or do it myself?

Handle the basics yourself: finish your Google Business Profile, ask for reviews and publish helpful pages. Competitive markets also need steady content, technical work and links, which most owners don't have time for.

Do I need SEO if I already run Google Ads?

They do different jobs. Ads buy visibility that stops the day you stop paying. SEO builds an asset that keeps bringing calls without a per-click cost. Many growing roofers run both.

What happens to my rankings if I stop paying for SEO?

Rankings don't vanish overnight, but they slide as competitors keep working. Make sure you own your content, accounts and website before you sign anything, so nothing is held hostage.
