Frequently Asked Questions

What makes SEO for roofers different from general local SEO?

Roofing searches are urgent and local at the same time. A homeowner with a leak is not browsing; they are calling the first credible roofer Google shows them in their town. General local SEO treats every business the same. Roofing SEO is built around service areas, repair and storm searches, reviews, and pages for every service and town you cover, because that is how roofing jobs are actually searched for. Miss any one of those, and you end up ranking for searches that do not book jobs in your market.

Do I need separate pages for each service and each town I serve?

Yes, if you want to be found for those searches. Google ranks pages, not businesses, so one homepage cannot carry "roof replacement in Evans" and "roof repair in Grovetown" at the same time. Each service and town page is another door a homeowner can walk through. It is why we build new local pages every month instead of treating your website as finished.

Can a small roofer outrank the big national lead companies?

In your own service area, yes. The national companies are strong everywhere and local nowhere: their pages are generic, and their reviews are spread across the whole country. Map results reward the roofer who is closest, best reviewed, and most clearly set up for that town. A small roofer with a tuned profile and steady reviews wins the calls closest to home.

How long does roofing SEO take to deliver leads?

There is no honest date. SEO builds as it goes: pages and listings go live first, then rankings and map visibility climb, and calls build on top of that. Competitive metro areas move slower, quieter towns faster. What you should see early is evidence, in pages going live and movement you can track, not promises. Anyone who guarantees you a date is selling you something.

Why is my SEO not generating calls?

Usually because rankings and calls are being treated as the same thing, and they are not. You can rank for searches nobody in your service area types, rank in towns you do not serve, or send homeowners to pages that never ask for the call. And if every call is not tracked to its source, you cannot even see which problem you have. The fix starts with measurement, then pages and a profile built around the searches that turn into estimates.

How much does roofing SEO cost?

It depends on the size of your market, how many towns you want to win, and how far behind your competitors you are starting from. A one-crew roofer in a small town and a multi-crew company in a metro area do not need the same engine. Be careful with flat packages priced before anyone looks at your market. The honest number comes after someone checks your rankings, your profile, and your competition, and that check is what the audit is for.

Can I do roofing SEO myself?

You can, and some owners do. The real cost is your time: your profile, your reviews, your pages, and your links all need attention every month, not once. Most owners start strong and stall by the second month, because the business needs them on roofs instead of on Google. If you do it yourself, judge it the same way you would judge us: by tracked calls, not by effort.

How do I know if my current SEO company is actually doing anything?

Ask for three things. The calls their work produced, tracked to the keyword or page that drove them. The actual pages and links they built this month, which you can open and read. And your rankings for the searches your customers really type. Real work leaves evidence you can see. If the report is all traffic charts and impressions, with no calls and nothing new built, you have your answer.
