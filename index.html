Good questions, straight answers.

What do you get in the review?

You’ll see where your site may be costing you calls, which local searches you could be missing, and the first fixes worth making. You can use the recommendations whether you hire help or handle them yourself.

Is this just a sales call?

No. You’ll answer a few questions about the jobs and areas you want more of, then walk through the findings. If help makes sense, you’ll see a clear next step. If it does not, you’ll still leave knowing what to fix.

Do you need a brand new website?

Not always. If your current site is fast, clear, and easy to update, a few focused changes may do the job. If it is confusing on a phone or hard for Google to understand, you’ll see why rebuilding may save time and money.

How do you prove the work is paying off?

Every website call is tracked and recorded. You see how many estimate requests came in, what each call cost, and which pages or searches produced them. Rankings help diagnose the work; calls, booked jobs, and revenue judge the result.

How quickly can you expect results?

A clearer website can help as soon as it launches. Local SEO usually takes longer and depends on your starting point and competition. The call log gives you an honest record of when new estimate opportunities begin showing up.

Will you own your website?

Yes. You should know where your site is hosted, what you own, and how to make changes. Those details will be clear before any work begins.

Is this only for roofers?

Roofers are the main focus, so the work stays tied to estimates, service areas, homeowner trust, and the jobs you want. A small number of other local service businesses may also be a fit.

What happens after your review?

If you want help, you’ll get a plain English scope with the work, price, and timeline. You decide whether the numbers make sense for your business, with no pressure and no hidden commitment.
