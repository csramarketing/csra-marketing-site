Frequently Asked Questions

Should I do SEO or Google Ads first for my roofing company?

For most roofers, ads first, with SEO started right behind them. Ads can put your number in front of homeowners this week, while rankings take months to build. The mistake is stopping there, because every month you only rent clicks, the meter keeps running. Run ads for work now, and build the pages that let you buy fewer clicks over time.

How quickly can Google Ads generate roofing leads compared to SEO?

Ads can start producing calls within days of a campaign going live, because you are paying to skip the line. SEO is slower: new pages need time to earn rankings, and the payoff builds over months, not days. The trade is speed now against cost later. Ads rent attention today; rankings keep sending calls after the spend stops.

Will running Google Ads help my SEO rankings?

No. Google keeps ads and organic rankings separate, and paying for clicks does not move your pages up. What ads can do is show you which keywords turn into real calls. That tells you exactly which pages are worth building first, so your SEO work goes where the money already is.

How much should a roofing company spend on Google Ads each month?

There is no honest flat number, because click costs swing hard by market and by keyword. A roofer in a quiet town and a roofer fighting ten competitors in a metro area are not playing the same game. Start from your real numbers instead: the calculator at the top of this page shows what your current clicks cost you each month. Your budget should be set by what a signed job is worth to you, not by a package someone sold you.

Is a $200 roofing lead too expensive?

It depends on what the lead turns into, so run it as a worked example. Say an average replacement job is $12,000 with a 40% gross margin, which is $4,800 in gross profit. If one in five $200 leads becomes a signed job, your cost per job is $1,000 against $4,800 in gross profit. The lead is not expensive or cheap on its own; your close rate decides that.

Why do shared leads end up costing more even though they are cheaper upfront?

A shared lead is sold to several roofers at once, so your real cost includes the estimates you run and lose to the other companies holding the same name. You also pay again next month for the same homeowner attention, because nothing about that lead belongs to you. A lead from your own pages costs you once to earn and keeps working. Cheap per lead often turns out expensive per signed job.

What happens to my leads when I stop paying for ads?

They stop. Paid clicks have no memory: the day the budget pauses, your ads disappear, and the calls they produced disappear with them. Rankings behave the opposite way. Pages you own stay in search, take clicks, and produce calls with no meter running. That difference is the whole reason this page exists.

Are Google Ads worth it for roofing companies?

Yes, when they are run as a bridge instead of a destination. Ads are the fastest way to get estimate requests while your rankings are still being built, and they prove which keywords make your phone ring. They turn into a bad deal when they are the only plan, because then you rent every job forever. Worth it means ads buy you time: you run them while your pages are being built, then pull spend back as the organic calls come in.
