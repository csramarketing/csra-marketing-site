Frequently Asked Questions

What does your marketing service actually cover?

Three things, built to work together. A website designed to turn visits into estimate requests. Local SEO that puts you in front of nearby homeowners searching for the work you want. And call tracking on every call the work produces, so the results are counted, not guessed at. Everything points at one outcome: exclusive estimate calls, yours alone, that you can tie back to the work.

Do you handle SEO, the website, my Google Business Profile, and reviews, or just some of those?

All of them, because they work or fail together. Your website and your local SEO are two of the three services on this page. Your Google Business Profile and your reviews sit inside the local SEO work: your profile is where map calls start, and reviews are the proof homeowners check before they call. Each piece supports the others, which is why the work covers all of it.

Do I need a new website, or can you work with the one I have?

It depends on what your current site can do. If it loads fast on a phone, shows your services, areas, and reviews clearly, and gives homeowners an easy way to call, it may carry the work. If it is slow, thin, or sends visitors hunting for your number, it will tax every other dollar you spend. The audit shows which side of that line your site is on, and whether targeted fixes would carry it, before you pay to rebuild anything.

How much does this cost?

There is no honest flat number, because the work scales with your market: how many towns you want to win, how strong the roofers ahead of you are, and how much building your site and rankings need. A one-crew company in a quiet town and a multi-crew company in a metro area are not buying the same engine. Be careful with anyone who prices it before looking at your market. The honest number comes after the audit, when your calls, your pages, and your competition have actually been checked.

How much should a roofing company spend on marketing?

Judge it against what a signed job returns, not against a percentage someone quotes you. Run it as a worked example: an average replacement job is $12,000, and at a 40% gross margin that is $4,800 in gross profit. Marketing that reliably produces signed jobs at a cost comfortably under that number is paying for itself. The right spend is the one where you can see the calls, count the jobs, and do that math every month.

How long until I start seeing results?

There is no honest date, and the pieces do not move at the same speed. Your website is the fast piece: it can be live and taking calls while the rankings are still being built. Rankings and map visibility climb behind it, and calls build on top of that. What you should see early is evidence, in pages going live and calls being tracked, not promises. Anyone who guarantees you a date is selling you something.

How do you show me the marketing is actually working?

Every call the website produces is logged and recorded, so the count of estimate opportunities is never a guess. You can divide the investment by the qualified calls and see your cost per opportunity, the same way you would judge a marketplace lead, except these calls are yours alone. And with the recordings plus your feedback on which estimates sold, calls get tied to booked jobs and revenue. If a month of work cannot show you that chain, it is activity, not proof.

Do you report on calls and revenue, or just traffic and rankings?

Calls and revenue. Traffic and rankings are signals, not the score: a ranking that never rings your phone changes nothing for your business. So the reporting starts with tracked calls and ends at the booked jobs those calls produced, not at where your pages sit on Google. That is the standard the work on this page is scored on.
