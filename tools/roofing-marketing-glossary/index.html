A Terms that start with A

Above the Fold

Website Design

Above the fold is everything a visitor sees on your website before they scroll down. On a phone, that is just the first screen, so it has to work hard. Your name, what you do, where you work, and a tap to call button should all live there, because most homeowners decide in seconds whether to stay or leave.

Related: Homepage, Call to Action (CTA), Mobile-Friendly

Algorithm

Local SEO

An algorithm is the set of rules Google uses to decide which websites show up first in search results. Google updates these rules often, which is why rankings can move up or down over time. Nobody outside Google knows the full recipe, but the basics never change: helpful pages, good reviews, and a fast, trustworthy website.

Related: Ranking, Ranking Factor, SEO

Alt Text

Website Design

Alt text is a short written description added to a photo on your website. Search engines cannot see pictures, so they read the alt text to understand what the photo shows. A photo of a finished roof in Augusta with alt text like "new shingle roof installed in Augusta, GA" also helps your site show up in image searches.

Related: SEO, On-Page SEO, Website

Analytics

Website Design + Local SEO

Analytics is the data that shows how people find and use your website. It tells you how many visitors you get, which pages they read, and how many of them call or send a form. Checking it monthly shows you what is working so you can do more of it. See also traffic and conversions.

Related: Traffic, Conversion, Google Search Console

Anchor Text

Local SEO

Anchor text is the clickable words in a link. When another site links to you with words like "Augusta roofing company," that text tells Google what your page is about. Links with clear, natural anchor text help your pages rank for the right searches. See backlinks.

Related: Backlink, Internal Link, Link Building

Authority

Local SEO

Authority is how much Google trusts your website as a real expert on roofing in your area. You build it with strong reviews, links from other trusted local sites, and helpful pages that answer homeowner questions. High authority sites rank higher and stay there longer.

Related: Backlink, Link Building, Trust Signals

B Terms that start with B

Backlink

Local SEO

A backlink is a link from someone else's website to yours. Google treats each quality backlink like a vote of confidence. A link from your local chamber of commerce or a supplier's dealer page counts far more than a link from a random site with nothing to do with roofing or your town. See link building.

Related: Link Building, Authority, Anchor Text

Blog

Website Design

A blog is the part of your website where you publish helpful articles, like "How to spot storm damage on your roof" or "Shingles vs. metal roofing: cost compared." Each post gives Google another page to rank and gives homeowners a reason to trust you before they ever pick up the phone. See content.

Related: Content, Keyword, Organic Traffic

Bounce Rate

Website Design

Bounce rate is the share of visitors who land on a page and leave without doing anything else. A high bounce rate often means the page loaded slowly, looked rough on a phone, or did not answer what the person searched for. Lowering it usually means more calls from the same traffic.

Related: Page Speed, User Experience (UX), Analytics

Brand

Website Design + Local SEO

Your brand is how people recognize and remember your company. It is your logo, your colors, your truck wrap, and the way your website and reviews make people feel about you. When a homeowner sees your name in search results and already knows it, they are far more likely to click you instead of a stranger.

Related: Website, Social Proof, Visibility

Breadcrumbs

Website Design

Breadcrumbs are the small row of links near the top of a page that shows the path home, like Home, then Services, then Roof Replacement. They help visitors find their way around, and they help Google understand how your site is organized. They matter most on bigger websites with many service and location pages.

Related: Navigation, Internal Link, Website

C Terms that start with C

Call to Action (CTA)

Website Design

A call to action is the button or line on a page that tells the visitor what to do next, like "Call now for a free inspection" or "Get my free roof estimate." Every page on your site needs one clear CTA. Pages without one get visitors. Pages with one get customers.

Related: Conversion, Landing Page, Click to Call

Call Tracking

Website Design + Local SEO

Call tracking records where your phone calls come from, so you know whether a caller found you on Google, your website, or somewhere else. It answers the most important question in marketing: what is actually making the phone ring? Without it, you are guessing which marketing is worth paying for. A website audit shows where calls leak, so you can see exactly which pages and listings are costing you jobs.

Related: Conversion, Analytics, Lead

Citation

Local SEO

A citation is any place online that lists your company name, address, and phone number, like Google, Yelp, Angi, or the BBB. Google compares these listings to check that your business is real and located where you say it is. When the details match everywhere, your local rankings get stronger. When they do not match, Google gets confused and trusts you less.

Related: NAP, Directory, Google Business Profile

Click to Call

Website Design

Click to call is a button on your mobile website that dials your number when a homeowner taps it. Most roofing searches happen on phones, often right after a storm or a leak, so removing every extra step matters. If calling you takes more than one tap, some of those homeowners will call the next company instead.

Related: Mobile-Friendly, Call to Action (CTA), Conversion

Click-Through Rate (CTR)

Local SEO

Click-through rate is how often people click your listing after seeing it in search results. If 100 people see your page on Google and 5 click it, your CTR is 5 percent. A clear title tag and a well written meta description raise your CTR, which brings more visitors without ranking any higher.

Related: Title Tag, Meta Description, Impression

Contact Form

Website Design

A contact form is the short set of boxes on your website where a homeowner types their name, phone number, and what they need, then sends it to you as a lead. Keep it short. Name, phone, address, and one box for the problem is plenty. Every extra field you add costs you submissions.

Related: Lead, Conversion, Landing Page

Content

Website Design + Local SEO

Content is everything on your website that a visitor can read, watch, or look at: your service pages, blog posts, photos, and videos. Good content answers the exact questions homeowners type into Google. Thin content, like a single paragraph that says you "do quality work," ranks for almost nothing.

Related: Blog, Service Page, Keyword

Conversion

Website Design

A conversion is when a visitor does the thing you wanted: calls you, sends the form, or books an inspection. It is the moment a stranger becomes a lead. Your whole website exists to create conversions, so every design choice should make calling or writing you easier.

Related: Conversion Rate, Call to Action (CTA), Lead

Conversion Rate

Website Design

Conversion rate is the share of visitors who turn into leads. If 200 people visit your site in a month and 10 call or send a form, your conversion rate is 5 percent. Small fixes, like a faster site, a clearer headline, and visible phone numbers, can double it without any extra traffic.

Related: Conversion, Analytics, Page Speed

Core Web Vitals

Website Design

Core Web Vitals are Google's scores for how fast and smooth your website feels to a real visitor. They measure how quickly the page loads, how soon you can tap things, and whether the page jumps around while loading. Google uses these scores as a ranking factor, and homeowners feel them even if they never see the numbers.

Related: Page Speed, Ranking Factor, User Experience (UX)

Crawl

Local SEO

Crawling is what Google does when it visits your website and reads your pages, following links from page to page like a spider on a web. If Google cannot crawl a page, that page cannot show up in search results. Broken links, blocked pages, and messy site structure can all leave good pages uncrawled. See indexing.

Related: Indexing, Sitemap, XML Sitemap

D Terms that start with D

Directory

Local SEO

A directory is a website that lists businesses by type and town, like Yelp, Angi, Houzz, or the BBB. Each listing is a citation that helps Google confirm your business details. Claim your listings, fill them out fully, and keep the name, address, and phone number identical to your Google Business Profile.

Related: Citation, NAP, Yelp

Domain Name

Website Design

Your domain name is your website's address on the internet, like yourcompany.com. Pick one that is short, easy to spell, and easy to say out loud on a job site or a radio ad. You own and pay for your domain yourself, and it stays with your company even if you change website providers.

Related: Website, URL, Hosting

Duplicate Content

Local SEO

Duplicate content is the same or nearly the same words appearing on more than one page, either on your own site or copied from someone else's. It confuses Google about which page to rank, so often neither page ranks well. This is why pasting the manufacturer's description on every location page with only the town name changed does not work. Each page needs its own real content.

Related: Content, Location Page, Indexing

E Terms that start with E

Engagement

Website Design + Local SEO

Engagement is how people interact with your business online: reading your pages, watching a video, tapping for directions, or calling from your Google listing. Strong engagement tells Google that homeowners find you useful, which supports your rankings. It also means more of your visitors turn into booked jobs.

Related: Google Business Profile, Analytics, Bounce Rate

External Link

Local SEO

An external link points from one website to a different website. When other sites link to you, that is a backlink. When you link out to a manufacturer or a local partner, you are giving an external link. Linking out to a trusted source now and then is normal and healthy. What matters most is the quality of the links pointing in.

Related: Backlink, Internal Link, Anchor Text

Back to the A to Z index

F Terms that start with F

Featured Snippet

Local SEO

A featured snippet is the short answer box that sometimes appears at the very top of Google, above the regular results. If a homeowner searches "how long does a shingle roof last," Google may pull a sentence from a website and show it right there. Winning the snippet puts your company above everyone else, even companies that rank first.

Related: Search Engine Results Page (SERP), Zero-Click Search, Content

Footer

Website Design

The footer is the block at the very bottom of every page on your website. It usually holds your phone number, address, service area, hours, and links to your main pages. A complete footer builds trust with visitors and repeats your NAP details where Google can read them on every page.

Related: NAP, Navigation, Website

Funnel

Website Design + Local SEO

A funnel is the path people take from first hearing about your company to hiring you, wide at the top and narrow at the bottom. At the top are people with a problem searching Google. In the middle are people comparing roofers and reading reviews. At the bottom are the ones who call. This glossary is top of funnel: it helps homeowners find and trust you early, before they ever need a roof.

Related: Journey, Lead Generation, Organic Traffic

G Terms that start with G

Google Business Profile

Local SEO

Your Google Business Profile is the free listing that controls what shows up when someone searches your company name or finds you on Google Maps. It shows your phone number, hours, photos, services, and reviews. For a roofer, this profile is often the first thing a homeowner sees, and it can ring your phone even if the person never visits your website. Keeping it complete, accurate, and full of fresh photos is the single highest value free marketing you can do.

Related: Google Maps, Review, Local Pack, NAP

Google Maps

Local SEO

Google Maps is where homeowners go to find a roofer near them, and it shows the local pack of three businesses at the top. Your spot on the map is driven by your Google Business Profile, your reviews, and how close you are to the person searching. If you are not on the map, you are invisible to the highest intent buyers in your area.

Related: Local Pack, Google Business Profile, Near Me Search

Google Posts

Local SEO

Google Posts are short updates you publish right on your Google Business Profile, like a finished project photo, a seasonal inspection offer, or a storm damage notice. They show your profile is active and cared for, and they give homeowners one more reason to pick you over a profile that has not been touched in two years.

Related: Google Business Profile, Engagement, Content

Google Reviews

Local SEO

Google reviews are the star ratings and written reviews customers leave on your Google Business Profile. They are often the deciding factor when a homeowner compares two roofers side by side. A steady flow of recent, genuine reviews beats a big pile of old ones, and replying to them shows you run a real, attentive company. See review response.

Related: Review, Review Response, Google Business Profile

Google Search Console

Local SEO

Google Search Console is Google's free report card for your website. It shows which searches bring people to your site, which pages Google has indexed, and any problems Google finds. It is the most honest data you can get about how your site is doing on Google, because it comes straight from Google.

Related: Analytics, Indexing, Impression

H Terms that start with H

Heading Tags

Website Design

Heading tags are the titles inside a page, labeled H1, H2, and H3 in the code. The H1 is the main headline, and H2s break the page into sections, like chapter titles in a book. Google reads your headings to understand what the page covers, and visitors skim them to decide if the page is worth reading. Every page gets one clear H1 that says what the page is and where you do it.

Related: Title Tag, On-Page SEO, Content

Homepage

Website Design

Your homepage is the front door of your website and usually your most visited page. It has one job: make it instantly clear who you are, what you do, where you work, and how to reach you. It should point visitors straight to your services, your reviews, and your phone number, not try to say everything at once.

Related: Above the Fold, Website, Navigation

Hosting

Website Design

Hosting is the service that stores your website's files and serves them to visitors, like rent for your spot on the internet. Cheap, overloaded hosting makes pages load slowly, and slow pages lose both visitors and rankings. You pay for your own hosting, so your website stays yours no matter who built it.

Related: Domain Name, Page Speed, Website

HTTPS

Website Design

HTTPS is the secure version of a web address. It means the connection between a visitor and your website is private and protected. Browsers label sites without it as "not secure," which scares homeowners away from sending you their name and address in a form. It is provided by an SSL certificate and Google expects every site to have it.

Related: SSL Certificate, Trust Signals, Website

I Terms that start with I

Impression

Local SEO

An impression is one time your business or website appears in search results, whether anyone clicks it or not. Impressions tell you how visible you are. If impressions climb but calls do not, your listing is showing up without winning the click, which usually points at your title tag, stars, or photos. See click-through rate.

Related: Click-Through Rate (CTR), Visibility, Google Search Console

Indexing

Local SEO

Indexing is Google saving your page into its library after crawling it. Only indexed pages can appear in search results. If a new service page is not indexed, it might as well not exist, which is why new pages are checked in Google Search Console after they launch.

Related: Crawl, Google Search Console, Sitemap

Internal Link

Website Design + Local SEO

An internal link connects one page of your website to another page of your website, like linking your roof repair page from a blog post about leaks. Internal links help visitors find the next step, help Google crawl your whole site, and pass strength from your popular pages to pages you want to rank. A page nobody links to is a page Google may never find.

Related: External Link, Crawl, Anchor Text

J Terms that start with J

Jobs

Website Design + Local SEO

Jobs here simply means booked roofing work: inspections, repairs, and replacements your company wins. Every term in this glossary connects back to jobs in the end. More visibility brings more leads, better pages turn more leads into booked inspections, and booked inspections turn into signed jobs. Marketing that cannot be traced to jobs is just spending.

Related: Lead, Lead Generation, Conversion

Journey

Website Design + Local SEO

The customer journey is the path a homeowner takes from first problem to booked job. It usually starts with a search like "roof leaking who to call," moves through reading reviews and comparing two or three websites, and ends with a call. Your website, Google profile, and reviews all have to show up along that path, because a gap anywhere loses the job to a competitor.

Related: Lead Generation, Google Reviews, Funnel

K Terms that start with K

Keyword

Local SEO

A keyword is the word or phrase someone types into Google, like "roof replacement Augusta GA" or "cost to fix a roof leak." Your pages should each target the keywords your real customers use, in the language they actually use. Homeowners search "roof repair near me," not "residential roofing solutions," so your pages should be written the way people search.

Related: Keyword Research, Search Volume, Long-Tail Keyword

Keyword Research

Local SEO

Keyword research is finding out exactly what homeowners in your area type into Google and how often. It shows which services have real demand, which questions people ask, and which searches your competitors are winning. Every good page starts here. Writing pages without it is guessing, and guessing is how websites end up ranking for searches nobody makes.

Related: Keyword, Search Volume, Content

Keyword Stuffing

Local SEO

Keyword stuffing is cramming a keyword into a page over and over in an unnatural way, like "best roofer Augusta best roofing company Augusta GA roofer." It worked in 2005. Today Google spots it, readers hate it, and it can drag your rankings down. Use your keyword where it naturally belongs, then write the rest of the page for the human reading it.

Related: Keyword, Content, Ranking Factor

Back to the A to Z index

L Terms that start with L

Landing Page

Website Design

A landing page is a page built for one purpose: turn visitors from a specific source, like a Google ad or a Facebook post, into leads. It focuses on one service, one offer, and one call to action, with nothing else to click and get distracted by. Focused pages convert far better than sending ad traffic to your homepage.

Related: Call to Action (CTA), Conversion, Pay-Per-Click (PPC)

Lead

Website Design + Local SEO

A lead is a potential customer who has given you a way to reach them: a phone call, a form, or a booked inspection. Leads are not jobs yet, but they are the raw material jobs are made from. Good marketing is judged on the quality and number of leads it produces, not on how pretty a website looks.

Related: Lead Generation, Conversion, Contact Form

Lead Generation

Website Design + Local SEO

Lead generation is everything you do to get homeowners to contact you: ranking on Google, running ads, earning reviews, and having a website that turns visitors into calls. For roofers, the strongest lead generation is local, because a lead three towns away in the wrong direction is worth little. Your Google profile, your map ranking, and your service pages do most of this work.

Related: Lead, Local SEO, Google Business Profile

Link Building

Local SEO

Link building is the work of earning backlinks from other websites to yours. Good link building means real local connections: chambers, associations, suppliers, community sponsorships, and local news. Bought links from random overseas sites are a fast way to get penalized. Slow, local, and real is what moves a roofer up the map and the results.

Related: Backlink, Authority, Citation

Local Pack

Local SEO

The local pack, also called the map pack, is the box of three local businesses Google shows with a small map at the top of local searches. It is the most valuable real estate in roofing marketing, because those three companies get most of the calls. Getting in comes down to your Google Business Profile, your reviews, and how well your website matches the search.

Related: Google Maps, Google Business Profile, Local SEO

Local SEO

Local SEO

Local SEO is the work of getting your roofing company found by people searching in your service area: on Google Maps, in the local pack, and in regular results for searches like "roofer near me." It combines your Google Business Profile, citations, reviews, and pages written for each town and service. Regular SEO chases the whole internet. Local SEO chases the homeowners within driving distance of your crews, which is the only traffic that pays.

Related: SEO, Google Business Profile, Citation, Location Page

Location Page

Local SEO

A location page is a page on your website built for one town you serve, like "Roof Replacement in Evans, GA." Done well, each page talks about real work in that town, local photos, and reviews from that area. Done lazily, it is the same paragraph with the town name swapped, which is duplicate content and ranks for nothing. See service pages.

Related: Duplicate Content, Service Area, Local SEO

Long-Tail Keyword

Local SEO

A long-tail keyword is a longer, more specific search phrase, like "how much does it cost to replace a roof on a 2000 square foot house." Fewer people search it, but the ones who do know exactly what they want and are close to hiring. Blog posts built on long-tail keywords are how smaller roofers outrank big national sites.

Related: Keyword, Keyword Research, Blog

M Terms that start with M

Meta Description

Local SEO

A meta description is the short summary under your page title in Google results. It does not directly raise your ranking, but it is your one sentence to convince a homeowner to click you instead of the company above you. Every important page needs one written by a human, with the service, the town, and a reason to call.

Related: Title Tag, Click-Through Rate (CTR), Meta Tags

Meta Tags

Local SEO

Meta tags are bits of code in a page that describe the page to search engines and social sites. The two homeowners ever see are the title tag and the meta description, shown in search results. They are small, invisible on the page itself, and they decide whether your listing gets clicked.

Related: Title Tag, Meta Description, On-Page SEO

Mobile-First Design

Website Design

Mobile-first design means the website is designed for phones first and adapted to bigger screens after. That order matters because most homeowners find you on a phone, often standing under a leak. If the site is built for desktop and squeezed onto a phone later, buttons end up tiny, text ends up small, and calls get lost.

Related: Mobile-Friendly, Responsive Design, Website

Mobile-Friendly

Website Design

Mobile-friendly means your website works well on a phone: text you can read without pinching, buttons a thumb can tap, and a call button that is always within reach. Google ranks mobile versions first, and homeowners searching after a storm are almost always on phones. A site that fights the visitor on mobile is a site that sends jobs to competitors.

Related: Mobile-First Design, Click to Call, Page Speed

N Terms that start with N

NAP

Local SEO

NAP stands for Name, Address, and Phone number, the core details of your business. Google checks that your NAP matches exactly across your website, your Google profile, and every directory. "Street" on one site and "St." on another, or an old phone number somewhere, quietly weakens your local rankings until it is cleaned up.

Related: Citation, Directory, Google Business Profile

Navigation

Website Design

Navigation is the menu that lets visitors move around your website. Keep it short and in plain words: Services, Reviews, About, Contact. If a homeowner cannot find your roof replacement page in two taps, the menu is decoration, not navigation. Google also follows your menu to understand which pages matter most.

Related: Breadcrumbs, Internal Link, Homepage

Near Me Search

Local SEO

A near me search is when someone searches "roofer near me" or "roof repair near me" instead of naming a town. Google answers using the searcher's location and the businesses closest to them with strong profiles. You cannot rank for near me searches across a whole state. You win them town by town, with your profile, reviews, and location pages doing the work.

Related: Google Maps, Local SEO, Location Page

O Terms that start with O

Off-Page SEO

Local SEO

Off-page SEO is everything done outside your website that affects your rankings: backlinks, citations, reviews, and mentions of your company around the web. Think of it as your reputation in town, while on-page SEO is the condition of your shop. Google weighs both before it ranks you.

Related: On-Page SEO, Link Building, Citation

On-Page SEO

Local SEO

On-page SEO is everything on your own pages that helps them rank: the right keywords, clear headings, a good title tag, helpful content, and internal links. It is the part of SEO fully in your control, and it is where every service and location page earns or loses its ranking.

Related: Off-Page SEO, Keyword, Title Tag

Organic Traffic

Local SEO

Organic traffic is visitors who find you through unpaid Google results, as opposed to ads you pay for per click. It builds slowly and then compounds: a page ranking first for "roof replacement" in your town brings leads month after month with no per click cost. This is the traffic SEO exists to grow.

Related: SEO, Traffic, Pay-Per-Click (PPC)

Back to the A to Z index

P Terms that start with P

Page Speed

Website Design

Page speed is how fast your website loads, especially on a phone with a weak signal. Every extra second costs you visitors. Most people leave if a page takes more than three seconds. Speed is a ranking factor, a trust signal, and the difference between a booked inspection and a homeowner who gave up and called someone else. See Core Web Vitals.

Related: Core Web Vitals, Hosting, Mobile-Friendly

Pay-Per-Click (PPC)

Website Design + Local SEO

Pay-per-click is advertising where you pay each time someone clicks your ad, like Google Ads at the top of search results. It brings leads fast, and the leads stop the day you stop paying. SEO is the opposite: slower to build, but the rankings keep working after the work is done. Most strong roofing companies use PPC for now and SEO for keeps.

Related: Organic Traffic, Landing Page, SEO

Portfolio

Website Design

Your portfolio is the gallery of finished roofs on your website: before and after photos, the town, and a line about the job. Real photos of your own work outperform any stock photo, because homeowners are checking whether you have done a roof like theirs, on a street like theirs. The portfolio is also quiet proof behind every claim your site makes.

Related: Social Proof, Service Page, Website

Primary Category

Local SEO

Your primary category is the main business type you pick on your Google Business Profile, and it should say "Roofing contractor." It is one of the strongest signals telling Google which searches to show you for. Pick the wrong primary category, and you can have a perfect profile that still does not show up when homeowners search for a roofer.

Related: Google Business Profile, Local Pack, Ranking Factor

Back to the A to Z index

Q Terms that start with Q

Q&A

Local SEO

Q&A is the questions and answers section on your Google Business Profile, where anyone can ask a question about your business. Homeowners ask about estimates, warranties, and service areas, and whatever sits unanswered is what the next homeowner reads. Answer your own common questions there first, in plain language, so the answers on your profile are yours.

Related: Google Business Profile, Engagement, Google Reviews

Back to the A to Z index

R Terms that start with R

Rank Tracking

Local SEO

Rank tracking is checking where your website and Google profile show up for your important searches, week after week. It shows whether your local SEO is climbing, flat, or slipping, town by town and keyword by keyword. Rankings move for many reasons, so the trend matters more than any single day's position.

Related: Ranking, Keyword, Analytics

Ranking

Local SEO

Your ranking is where you show up in Google results for a given search. Position one gets the most calls, and positions past the first page get almost none. Rankings differ by the search, the town, and even the street the searcher is standing on, which is why "we rank number one" only means something with a keyword and a place attached to it.

Related: Rank Tracking, Ranking Factor, Algorithm

Ranking Factor

Local SEO

A ranking factor is anything Google weighs when deciding search results: your reviews, your links, your page content, your distance from the searcher, and hundreds more. No single factor wins alone. Local rankings for roofers mostly come down to a complete Google profile, plenty of recent reviews, and pages that match what homeowners search for.

Related: Ranking, Algorithm, Google Business Profile

Responsive Design

Website Design

Responsive design means your website automatically reshapes itself to fit any screen: phone, tablet, or desktop. The text stays readable, the photos resize, and the call button stays easy to tap. One website serves every visitor well, instead of a phone version and a computer version drifting apart.

Related: Mobile-Friendly, Mobile-First Design, User Experience (UX)

Review

Local SEO

A review is a customer's public rating and comments about your company, most importantly on Google. Reviews do three jobs at once: they convince homeowners, they feed your map rankings, and they tell Google your business is active and trusted. The roofer with 200 recent reviews beats the roofer with 40 old ones almost every time.

Related: Google Reviews, Review Response, Social Proof

Review Response

Local SEO

A review response is your public reply to a customer review. Replying to every review, good and bad, shows homeowners you are paying attention, and it gives you the last word on the rare unfair one. Keep responses short and professional. Future customers are the real audience, not the reviewer.

Related: Review, Google Reviews, Google Business Profile

Rich Results

Local SEO

Rich results are search listings with extras attached, like gold stars showing your rating, or answers pulled directly into the results. They take up more space and win more clicks than a plain blue link. Stars on your listings come from your reviews plus schema markup telling Google where to find them.

Related: Schema Markup, Featured Snippet, Review

Back to the A to Z index

S Terms that start with S

Schema Markup

Local SEO

Schema markup is a bit of code added to your website that labels your business details for Google: your name, address, phone, hours, services, and reviews. Homeowners never see it, but Google reads it to understand your business with confidence. It helps your listings earn stars and other rich results instead of plain text.

Related: Rich Results, NAP, On-Page SEO

Search Engine

Local SEO

A search engine is a tool like Google that catalogs websites and answers searches with a ranked list of results. Google handles the overwhelming majority of searches, including nearly every "roofer near me" typed in the CSRA, so it is the only search engine most local marketing is built for.

Related: SEO, Search Engine Results Page (SERP), Google Maps

Search Engine Results Page (SERP)

Local SEO

The SERP is the page Google shows after someone searches. For a roofing search it is a stack of ads, the map pack, organic results, and sometimes videos or answer boxes. Every part of your marketing is fighting for a spot on this one page, and the spots are not equal. The map pack and the top organic spots take nearly all the calls.

Related: Local Pack, Featured Snippet, Organic Traffic

Search Intent

Local SEO

Search intent is what the person actually wants when they type a search. "Roof replacement cost" means they want prices and education. "Roofer near me" means they want to hire someone now. A page wins when it matches the intent behind the search, so cost questions get cost answers, and hiring searches land on pages that make calling easy.

Related: Keyword, Search Engine Results Page (SERP), Landing Page

Search Volume

Local SEO

Search volume is how many times a keyword gets searched in a set time, usually per month. It tells you whether a page is worth building. A service with real volume in your towns deserves its own page. A phrase nobody searches does not, no matter how clever it sounds. See keyword research.

Related: Keyword Research, Keyword, Long-Tail Keyword

SEO

Local SEO

SEO stands for search engine optimization. It is the work of getting your website and Google profile to show up higher when homeowners search for roofing services, without paying for each click. It is not a trick or a one time fix. It is a steady system of strong pages, accurate listings, real reviews, and trusted links that compounds month after month. For our full breakdown, see local SEO, the version built for companies that serve a local area.

Related: Local SEO, On-Page SEO, Off-Page SEO

Service Area

Local SEO

Your service area is the set of towns and neighborhoods your crews actually cover. Listing it accurately on your Google profile and website tells Google where to show you and tells homeowners you work on their street. Claim too wide an area and you dilute your strength everywhere. Claim the towns you truly serve and dominate those.

Related: Location Page, Google Business Profile, Local SEO

Service Page

Website Design

A service page is a page dedicated to one service, like roof replacement, roof repair, or storm damage restoration. One page per service lets each page target its own keyword and answer that buyer's exact questions about process, cost, and timing. A single "services" page trying to cover everything usually ranks for none of it.

Related: Location Page, Keyword, Content

Sitemap

Local SEO

A sitemap is a list of every important page on your website, handed to Google so nothing gets missed. It is a map of your site in the literal sense. When a new service or location page launches, the sitemap is how Google finds it quickly instead of whenever it happens to wander by. See XML sitemap.

Related: XML Sitemap, Crawl, Indexing

Social Proof

Website Design + Local SEO

Social proof is evidence that other homeowners already hired you and were glad they did: reviews, star ratings, project photos, and testimonials. People trust strangers less than they trust the experience of neighbors. Putting proof on every page, next to every claim, is what turns a nice website into one that books inspections.

Related: Review, Testimonial, Portfolio

SSL Certificate

Website Design

An SSL certificate is what gives your website the padlock icon and the "https" at the start of its address. It encrypts the information visitors send you, like their name and address in an estimate form. Without one, browsers warn visitors your site is not secure, and most homeowners will simply leave. See HTTPS.

Testimonial

Website Design

A testimonial is a customer's words about working with you, published on your website with their name and town. Title Tag

Local SEO

The title tag is the clickable headline of your page in Google results, written in the page's code. It is one of the strongest signals of what a page is about, so it should carry the service and the town, like "Roof Replacement in Augusta, GA." It is also the first impression a homeowner gets of your company in search, often before your name.

Traffic

Website Design + Local SEO

Traffic is the flow of visitors to your website, usually counted per month. More traffic is only good if it is the right traffic: homeowners in your service area with a roofing need. Website Design + Local SEO

Trust signals are the details on your website and profiles that make a homeowner comfortable calling you: licensing and insurance mentions, real reviews, your address and phone number, years in business, and photos of your actual crew and work. Strangers hand you their home based on these signals. Missing ones cost you calls you never hear about.

Website Design

Website Design

Bad UX is a menu that hides services, text too small to read, and a form that asks for everything except a blood sample.

Roofing Marketing Questions, Answered Simply

What is local SEO for a roofing company?

Local SEO is the work of getting your roofing company found by homeowners searching in the towns you serve. It covers your Google Business Profile, your map ranking, your reviews, your listings across directories, and pages built for each service and town. It matters because nearly every roofing search has local intent: the homeowner wants a crew that can actually come to their house.

What is the difference between a website and a Google Business Profile?

Your Google Business Profile is the free listing that shows your phone number, hours, reviews, and location on Google and Maps. Your website is the full home base you own, with your services, projects, and story. The profile gets you seen and called. The website convinces the homeowner who wants to check you out first. Strong roofing companies run both at full strength.

How long does SEO take to work for a roofer?

Most roofing companies see clear movement in rankings and calls within 3 to 6 months, because SEO compounds: every strong page, review, and link keeps working after it is in place. Quick wins like a cleaned up Google profile and fixed listings can show results in the first few weeks. Anyone promising page one in a week is selling something that will not last.

Do I need a blog on my roofing website?

A blog is one of the best ways to answer the exact questions homeowners type into Google, like what a new roof costs or how to spot storm damage. Each post is another page that can rank and another reason for a homeowner to trust you before calling. A few genuinely helpful posts a quarter will outperform a pile of thin ones.
