Chapter 01

Regular SEO Is About the Best Answer. Local SEO Is About the Closest One.

Regular SEO is what most people mean when they say "SEO." It's Google trying to answer a question with the best page on the internet.

"How long does a roof last?" That's a regular SEO question. The best answer wins, no matter where in the country it was written.

Local SEO is a different game. The question isn't "what's the best answer?" It's "who can actually show up at my house?"

A roofer in Texas can't fix a roof in Georgia. Google knows that. So for local searches, Google adds a whole second layer of ranking rules on top of everything regular SEO already cares about.

Why?

Because when someone's ceiling is leaking, they don't need the world's best article about roof repair. They need a roofer who is close, open, and picking up the phone right now.

This one difference changes everything about how you should think about your marketing. Proximity isn't a footnote in local SEO. It's the foundation.

Chapter 02

The 3 Things Google Looks At (In Roofer Terms)

Google has published exactly what it evaluates for local searches. Three factors: relevance, distance, and prominence.

Relevance is about match. Does your business actually do what the person asked for? If someone searches "metal roof installation" and you only install shingles, you're not relevant to that search.

Distance is about proximity. How close is your business to the person searching? Google measures this literally, using the searcher's location.

Prominence is about trust. How well known and well regarded is your business? Reviews, mentions across the web, and how established you appear all feed into this.

Regular SEO mostly cares about the quality of your content. Local SEO cares about all of that plus where you are, what you actually do, and whether real customers vouch for you.

Most roofers I talk to are only playing half this game. They either have a decent website and ignore the local signals entirely, or they have reviews and a profile but a website that can't convert a visitor. You need both halves working together.

Chapter 03

Why "Roofer Near Me" Doesn't Behave Like Other Searches

Type "best running shoes" into Google and you get articles, stores, and ads from all over the country.

Type "roofer near me" and something completely different happens. You get a map. Three businesses. Phone numbers front and center, one tap away.

That map box is called the map pack, and it captures the majority of clicks on local searches. (BrightLocal's annual Local Consumer Review Survey has documented this pattern consistently.)

The person searching isn't browsing for fun. They're not comparing ten options over two weeks. They have a problem right now and they want someone who can fix it soon.

Bottom line?

Local SEO isn't about winning the internet. It's about winning a radius, usually somewhere in the range of 5 to 15 miles around your shop. (Typical market range. Your exact radius depends on how competitive your market is and how many roofers are fighting for the same searches.)

Chapter 04

Your Google Business Profile Is Your Second Homepage

Before a homeowner ever visits your website, they see your Google Business Profile. It's the listing with your reviews, photos, hours, and phone number that shows up in the map pack and in Maps.

For a roofer, this profile often gets more views than the actual website in many markets. That's not an exaggeration. When someone searches "roofer near me," the profile is what they see first, and many callers never click through to a website at all.

Reviews live here. Photos of your finished work live here. Your phone number lives here, one tap away from a booked job.

Why?

Because Google trusts what real customers say about you more than what you say about yourself. A profile with far more detailed reviews will consistently outperform a thin one, all else being equal.

I'm not going to walk you through optimizing the profile step by step here. That's a separate job with its own playbook. But understand this clearly: if your profile is thin, outdated, or full of unanswered reviews, your local SEO is fighting with one hand tied behind its back.

Chapter 05

Why Generic SEO Advice Fails Roofers

Search "SEO tips" and you'll drown in advice. Most of it was written for blogs, ecommerce stores, and software companies. Almost none of it was written for a roofer with a defined service area.

Generic advice says: publish more content. For a roofer, 200 blog posts about shingle types won't move the needle if your local signals are weak. Content helps, but it's not the lever that matters most locally.

Generic advice says: build backlinks from big websites. For a roofer, a link from a local supplier, a chamber of commerce, or a community sponsorship page is worth more than a link from a national blog that nobody in your market has ever heard of.

Generic advice says: target high-volume keywords. For a roofer, "roof replacement cost" typed by someone 400 miles away is worthless to you. "Roof replacement" typed by someone 4 miles away is worth a $12,000 job in a typical replacement scenario.

Here's the deal.

Roofing is a local business with a local buyer and a local Google. Advice built for national websites will waste your time and your money.

Chapter 06

What Good Local SEO Looks Like for a Roofer

So what does it actually look like when local SEO is working for a roofing company? Five things, and you can check every one of them yourself.

  • You show up in the map pack for your core services across your service area. Not once in a while when Google feels generous. Consistently, week after week.
  • Your profile and your website tell the same story. Same services, same service areas, same phone number. Google cross-checks everything, and inconsistencies cost you.
  • Reviews keep coming in, and you respond to all of them. Not just the five-star ones. Especially not just the five-star ones. How you handle a bad review tells future customers more than the review itself.
  • Your website has a real page for every service and every area you serve. Not one thin "services" page trying to rank for everything. Real pages with real content about real work.
  • Your business name, address, and phone number are identical everywhere they appear online. Directories, social profiles, your website footer. Inconsistencies confuse Google's trust signals.

That's the what and the why. The how behind each of these is a longer conversation, and honestly, it's one worth having with someone who does this specifically for roofers rather than figuring it out alone.

Frequently asked questions

Is local SEO the same as regular SEO?

No. Regular SEO is about ranking the best content for a question, regardless of location. Local SEO adds proximity, relevance, and prominence on top, because Google needs to know you can physically serve the person searching. For roofers, local SEO is the game that actually matters.

Do I still need a website if I have a Google Business Profile?

Yes. Your profile gets you seen, but your website gets you chosen. Homeowners click through to look at your finished work, read your reviews in detail, and decide whether you look legitimate. A strong profile paired with a weak website leaks leads at the exact moment they were ready to call.

How much does local SEO cost for a roofing company?

Most roofing companies invest somewhere between $1,500 and $3,000 per month for ongoing local SEO work. (Typical market range.) One-time projects like a full website rebuild or a profile overhaul are priced separately. Here is a full breakdown of how much roofing SEO costs.

Can I do local SEO myself?

You can handle the basics on your own: claiming your profile, asking happy customers for reviews, and keeping your business information consistent everywhere. The ongoing work, content, technical fixes, and authority building, takes real hours every single month. Most owners run out of hours long before they run out of tasks.

How do I know if my local SEO is working?

Watch three things: whether you appear in the map pack for your core keywords, how many calls come from your profile and website each month, and whether your cost per lead trends down over time. Rankings alone don't pay for trucks. Calls do.

What's the difference between organic results and the map pack?

The map pack is the box with the map and three business listings that appears at the top of local searches. Organic results are the traditional blue links below it. For "near me" searches, the map pack sits above everything and captures the majority of clicks, which is why it matters so much for roofers.

Does my service area actually matter for SEO?

Enormously. Google ranks you partly based on your proximity to the searcher, so a roofer in one town won't automatically rank in the next town over. If you serve multiple areas, each one needs its own visibility strategy, starting with dedicated pages on your website.
