19 Ways to Get More Roofing Leads in 2027 (Beyond Word of Mouth)

Written by Kemar Lawrence, Co-Founder, CSRA Marketing
Published Oct. 20, 2026 · Last updated Oct. 20, 2026
19 lead channels for roofers, ranked by what actually works in 2027.

If word of mouth is your whole lead pipeline, you do not really have a pipeline. You have luck on a schedule you do not control.

Below are 19 ways to get more roofing leads in 2027, ranked by how well each one actually works for a local roofing company: lead quality, speed to first result, and how much of the channel you actually control. Every claim is linked to its research so you can check it yourself. One thing to notice as you read: these channels split into two kinds, the ones you rent and the ones you own. Most roofers only run the rented ones.

Start at number 1 and work down. The top five alone will outproduce every referral you are waiting on.

Here is the tension in your market right now: 79% of homeowners say they find a roofer through word of mouth, but 62% also search online and 95% weigh online reviews before they call (Roofing Contractor, 2025 Homeowner Roofing Survey). The roofers winning in 2027 stack both. This list shows you how.

1. Optimize your Google Business Profile

Your Google Business Profile is the free listing that shows up in the map pack when a homeowner searches "roofer near me." Claim it, pick "Roofing contractor" as your primary category, list every service, set your service areas, and load it with photos of real jobs.

Why?

Because this is where local demand actually lives. BrightLocal's study of more than 45,000 listings found the average business gets found in 1,009 searches per month, and 84% of those are discovery searches from people who never typed the business name.

Google's own figures say listings with photos get 42% more requests for driving directions and 35% more clicks to the website. A half finished profile is a lead leak you can fix this week.

2. Build a reviews engine, not a reviews hope

Ask every customer, every time: in person at the final walkthrough, then again by email and text with a direct link to your review page. Then respond to every review, good or bad, within a few days. Reviews are not decoration. They are the second opinion every homeowner checks before calling you.

The numbers back the system up. BrightLocal's 2025 Local Consumer Review Survey found only 4% of consumers say they never read reviews, and email is the ask that works best: 40% of consumers say they are most likely to leave a review when asked by email, ahead of 27% in person.

63% expect a response within two to three days to a week. And in Roofing Contractor's 2025 homeowner survey, 95% of homeowners rated reviews important when choosing a roofer.

3. Your own website plus local SEO (the asset that compounds)

Build a dedicated page on your own website for every service you sell and every town you serve: replacement, repair, metal, storm damage. Each page targets the searches homeowners actually type, like "roofing company" plus your city. This is the channel I would fund first if I were starting from zero, and it is not close.

Here is why. Backlinko's analysis of roughly 4 million Google results found the number 1 organic result averages a 27.6% click through rate, the top 3 results collect 54.4% of all clicks, and only 0.63% of searchers ever click a page 2 result. Position is the whole game.

And the demand is real: 62% of homeowners say they search online to find a roofing contractor. Unlike ads, a ranking page keeps sending you calls after you stop paying for it. That is what compounding looks like. It is also why I tell every roofer to compare what they spend renting attention against what it costs to own it. My lead cost comparison tool breaks the channels down side by side, and my local SEO page shows what a real local campaign includes.

4. Referral partnerships with realtors, agents, and property managers

Word of mouth is powerful. Systematized word of mouth is better. Build standing relationships with the people who hear about roof problems first: realtors whose listings fail inspection, insurance agents right after a claim, property managers running portfolios. Give them one named contact at your company, a fast response promise, and send work back their way when you can.

Nielsen's Trust in Advertising study found 88% of consumers trust recommendations from people they know more than any other channel, and Roofing Contractor's survey found 79% of homeowners find a roofer through referrals or recommendations, more than any other method.

Partnerships put your name inside that trust on purpose instead of by accident.

5. Past customer reactivation

Your cheapest leads are already in your phone. Work the list of everyone you have ever roofed or quoted: a seasonal check in, a free post-storm inspection offer, a simple referral ask a few times a year. These homeowners already trust your work, and their roofs keep aging whether you stay in touch or not.

Roofing Contractor's 2025 survey found 54% of homeowners contact firms they have used before when they need a roofer, the third most common way homeowners find one, and 61% of roofing contractors say they actively service repeat clients.

If your past customer list is sitting in a spreadsheet doing nothing, that is revenue on pause.

6. Google Local Services Ads

Local Services Ads are Google's screened listings. You apply, Google checks your license, insurance, and background, and your company shows up in the box at the very top of the results page with your star rating attached, above the regular ads. The model is different from normal ads: you pay when a homeowner contacts you through the ad, not when they click it.

Why do roofers like it? Because the trust screening does half the selling before the phone rings. A homeowner comparing three companies at the top of Google sees one that Google itself has vetted.

Set a weekly budget you are comfortable with, answer fast, and dispute any lead that is not a real roofing job in your area. It pairs well with number 7, but many roofers start here because the intent is as high as search gets.

7. Google Ads on high intent searches

Google Ads lets you bid on the searches that mean money right now: "roof replacement" plus your city, "roof repair near me," "emergency roof leak." Send those clicks to a dedicated page with a call button and a short quote form, not your homepage.

Go in with your eyes open on cost. Search Engine Land's report on the WordStream by LocaliQ 2025 benchmarks, covering more than 16,000 campaigns, puts the average cost per lead across all industries at $70.11, with home improvement among the most expensive clicks at $7 or more each.

Roofing runs hotter than average, which is exactly why you judge this channel on cost per booked job, not cost per click. One booked replacement covers a lot of clicks.

8. A quote follow up system for old estimates

Every unsold estimate is a lead you already paid for. Put each one on a schedule: a call or text within days of the quote, then touches at two weeks, thirty days, and right after the next storm rolls through. Most roofers quote and vanish. The job goes to whoever is still talking to the homeowner.

Roofing Contractor's survey explains why this works: when homeowners pick between two roofing companies, 67% say better communication is the deciding factor, and 40% call poor communication the biggest challenge of working with a roofer, ahead of workmanship at 25% and pricing at 18%.

Run your own math on it: if you write 20 estimates a month and close 8 of them, that is 12 paid for leads going quiet every 30 days (worked example, adjust it to your real numbers). A follow up system works that list before you spend another dollar on ads. You do not need more leads to win these jobs. You need to still be in the conversation when they decide.

9. Short video content from real jobs

Film 30 to 60 second phone videos on the job: the tear off, the rot you found under the shingles, the finished roof from the street. Add a few explainers, like three signs your roof is failing. Post them to your Google profile, your website, and your social pages. No studio, no script, no acting.

More than three quarters of US consumers watch video when researching local businesses, and the type they prefer most is video the business itself posted (BrightLocal). Marketers are leaning the same direction: HubSpot reports short form video is the highest ROI content format at 49% and the most used format overall.

A homeowner who has watched you explain a roof problem already trusts you before the estimate.

10. Before and after photo content

Photograph every job three times: before, during, and after. Publish the sets everywhere you exist: Google profile, project pages on your website, social feeds, even a printed folder you open at the kitchen table. Roofing is visual. A homeowner cannot see your workmanship, so show it.

Google's own numbers say this matters more than most roofers think: businesses with photos on their listing receive 42% more requests for driving directions and 35% more clicks to their website than businesses without photos (BrightLocal's Google Business Profile Insights Study).

11. Facebook and local community groups

Join the neighborhood, HOA, and "what's happening" groups for the towns you serve. Answer roof questions helpfully, post job photos with permission, and be the name residents recognize before the storm ever hits. Do not pitch in every thread. Be useful, be local, be patient.

Roughly a quarter of homeowners use social media to find a roofing contractor, and among millennial homeowners it is 40% (Roofing Contractor 2025).

Sixty percent of roofing contractors already promote their business on social media, which means staying silent makes your absence the loudest thing about you.

12. Storm response canvassing

When hail or wind tears through a neighborhood, get your crews into that area within days offering free, documented inspections while damage is fresh and insurance claim windows are open. This is the one channel where a month of demand appears overnight.

The scale of it is bigger than most owners realize. NOAA's Storm Prediction Center database holds roughly 346,000 severe hail reports going back to 1955, and US insured losses from severe convective storms, mostly hail, have topped $10 billion every year since 2008.

Storm work is not a windfall. It is a permanent, recurring demand cycle in this industry, and the roofers who treat it as a system instead of a surprise eat first.

13. Email marketing to your own list

Every past customer and every unsold quote belongs on an email list you own. Send something genuinely useful a few times a year: a storm season checklist, a post-storm inspection offer, a friendly referral request. No blasting, no buying lists, no weekly noise.

Why does a simple list earn a spot this high? Because nobody can throttle it, raise its price, or change its algorithm. Your Google rankings can wobble and your ad costs can spike, but an email to a homeowner who already knows your name costs you almost nothing and lands the same morning.

The list also feeds everything above it: it is the easiest place to ask for the reviews from channel 2 and reopen the estimates from channel 8.

14. Local sponsorships

Put your name on the Little League outfield fence, the high school scoreboard, the charity golf hole. Homeowners hire names they recognize, and sponsorship parks your name in front of the same families for years at a cost that barely registers next to one ad campaign.

BrightLocal's researchers flag sponsorships and community involvement as an overlooked local visibility play, and their survey work found 48% of US adults turn to local news outlets for local business information, a reminder that local attention still compounds locally.

This channel will never show up neatly in a spreadsheet. Its job is simpler: when the storm hits and three trucks are on the street, yours is the name that already feels familiar.

15. Strategic alliances with other trades

Formalize two way referrals with the trades around you: gutter, siding, solar, window, and painting companies, plus remodelers who do not roof. Their customers need roofs. Your customers need their trades. Agree on how handoffs work, who calls whom, and actually track what gets sent each way.

The mechanism is borrowed trust. Nielsen found 88% of consumers trust recommendations from people they know more than any other channel, and an introduction from a contractor the homeowner already hired carries that same weight straight into the kitchen.

A siding crew that just finished a house with a tired roof is the warmest introduction in this business.

16. Maintenance and inspection offers

Offer a free or low cost annual roof inspection to past customers and to homeowners in your older neighborhoods. Every inspection is a sales conversation with someone whose roof is quietly wearing out, plus a fresh chance at a review and a referral while you are standing in their driveway.

Homeowners come back to companies they know: 54% contact firms they have used before when they need roofing work (Roofing Contractor 2025).

An inspection program manufactures those return visits on purpose instead of hoping the homeowner remembers your name in year nine. Find a small repair today and you are the only call they make when the replacement comes due.

17. Pricing transparency content on your website

Publish honest pricing guidance on your site: what a replacement typically runs by home size and material, what drives the price up or down, and a simple way to estimate a range. It captures the highest intent searches in your market, the "roof replacement cost" searches homeowners type right before they start calling.

Here is the widest open lane in the industry right now. Roofing Contractor's 2025 survey found 78% of homeowners are more likely to call a contractor who offers pricing on their website, up from 66% in 2023, yet only about a quarter of contractors publish any pricing at all.

Your competitors are hiding their numbers. Publishing guidance, not a quote, pre-sells the homeowner before your phone ever rings. I break down what a website that does this job actually costs in my guide to roofing website cost.

18. Nextdoor

Claim your free business page on Nextdoor, then earn recommendations in the exact neighborhoods you work. Nextdoor runs on neighbors telling neighbors who to hire, which makes it word of mouth with an address attached and a memory that does not fade.

Play it like the groups in channel 11: be helpful first, answer the "anyone know a good roofer" threads fast, and ask happy customers on the street you just finished to drop you a recommendation there.

One strong thread in a big neighborhood can feed you for a season, because the recommendation stays searchable long after the conversation ends.

19. Direct mail postcards

Mail oversized postcards to targeted neighborhoods: the streets you just roofed on, subdivisions full of aging roofs, and storm paths right after an event. One postcard is a flyer. The same street hearing from you three or four times a year is a presence.

Why does old school mail still make the list? Because it reaches the homeowners your digital channels miss: the ones who are not searching yet, not scrolling, not in the groups. The roof is aging either way.

A postcard with a free inspection offer gives them a reason to look up, and when they do, your crews, your signs, and your reviews are already familiar. Repetition matters more than design flair here.

Where I would start if I were you

You do not need all 19 on day one. Start with the free owned channels: your Google profile (1), your reviews engine (2), your website and its service pages (3). Add the follow up system (8), because those leads are already paid for. Then layer in paid speed (6 and 7) when the pipeline needs pressure.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best way to get roofing leads?

A stack, not a single channel. Your Google Business Profile, a real reviews engine, and your own website with service pages for each town you serve will outproduce any one tactic, because 62% of homeowners search online and 95% weigh reviews before calling. Layer paid channels like Google Ads on top when you need leads faster than the owned channels can build.

How can I get roofing leads fast?

Paid search is the fastest switch you can flip. Google Local Services Ads and Google Ads on high intent searches like roof replacement plus your city can put you in front of ready homeowners within days. The fastest free source is your own history: past customers and unsold estimates already know you, and a follow up call this week can book work a new campaign would take a month to find.

How much does roofing lead generation cost?

It ranges from free to expensive depending on the channel. Your Google profile, reviews, and past customer list cost time, not money. Paid search costs real budget: across more than 16,000 campaigns, the average Google Ads cost per lead was $70.11 in 2025, and roofing runs above that average. Judge every channel on cost per booked job. One $12,000 roof at a 40% margin is $4,800 in gross profit, which buys a lot of clicks before the math stops working (worked example).

How long does roofing SEO take to start producing leads?

Plan on months, not weeks. Rankings build as Google learns your pages deserve the clicks, and the payoff for patience is steep: the number 1 result averages a 27.6% click through rate while page 2 results get clicked by only 0.63% of searchers. Most roofers pair SEO with one paid channel so the pipeline has leads now and a compounding asset later.

Are Facebook ads worth it for roofing companies?

They can be, with the right expectations. About a quarter of homeowners use social media to find a roofer, and among millennial homeowners it is 40%, so your buyers are there. Social works best for staying visible, retargeting people who visited your site, and storm response in a defined area. For pure ready to buy today intent, Google searches still beat the feed.

Are bought leads from Angi or HomeAdvisor worth it?

Sometimes, as a supplement. The catch is that marketplace leads are usually shared with several competitors, so you are racing to the phone and competing on price against whoever else bought the same name. Many roofers start there, then shift budget toward channels they own once they see the math. I compare the two paths head to head in my Angi vs. your own website breakdown.

Do blogs and content actually help a roofing company get leads?

Yes, when the content answers what homeowners actually search. Pricing guidance is the clearest proof: 78% of homeowners say they are more likely to call a contractor who publishes pricing, and only about a quarter of roofers do it. Pages like that rank, get found, and pre-sell the call. Content is not a branding exercise. It is your website doing sales work while you are on a roof.
