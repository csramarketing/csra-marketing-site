Chapter 01

The short answer: yes, with a catch

Roofing SEO works. I have seen it take companies from invisible to the top of the map pack. I have also seen roofers pay for two years and get nothing.

Here's the deal: the difference is never the strategy. It is the execution and the expectations.

Most roofers get sold a fantasy: rankings in 30 days, page one guaranteed. When that does not happen, they conclude SEO does not work. The promise was fake, not the channel.

SEO is a compounding asset. Paid ads and lead marketplaces rent you attention. SEO builds you something you own. That is the honest case for it, and it is a strong one. Our local SEO service is built entirely around that compounding idea.

Chapter 02

What "working" actually looks like (it is not rankings)

Ask a roofer what SEO success looks like and they say "rank number one." That is the wrong target.

Rankings are a means. Calls are the end. I would rather rank third and get eight calls a week than rank first and get two. The only metric that pays your crew is booked jobs.

Here is what a working campaign produces, in order. First, your Google Business Profile starts showing up in the map pack for your main service plus city searches. Then your phone starts ringing from people who found you on Google, not from ads. Then those calls turn into inspections, and inspections turn into signed contracts.

Track this weekly: how many calls came from organic search? If that number climbs month over month, your SEO works. If your agency reports rankings but cannot show calls, you are paying for a report, not growth.

Chapter 03

The honest timeline: 4 to 8 months to real traction

This is the part most agencies will not tell you straight. SEO takes months. Anyone who says otherwise is selling you something.

Here is the realistic timeline I give every roofer (illustrative timeline based on typical campaign patterns):

Months 1 to 2: foundation. Site fixes, profile optimization, review strategy starts. You will see almost nothing. That is normal.

Months 3 to 4: early signals. Some keywords move. A few extra calls trickle in. Most roofers get impatient here. Do not.

Months 5 to 8: traction. The curve bends. Organic calls become a real line item. If you see nothing meaningful by month 8, the campaign is broken, not SEO.

Months 9 to 14: compounding. Earlier work keeps paying while new work stacks on top.

Bottom line? Plan on 4 to 8 months before a campaign hits its stride. If someone promises faster, ask what they will do in 30 days that takes everyone else six months.

Chapter 04

The math: when SEO beats buying leads

Let me put numbers on this so you can check my work.

Say you spend $1,500 a month on SEO (typical market range for a real local campaign is $1,500 to $5,000 a month; below that range you are usually buying automated reports, not actual work). Over 12 months that is $18,000.

Now compare that to buying shared leads at, say, $75 each (typical market range for roofing leads is $50 to $120 per lead depending on market and platform). Twenty leads a month costs you $1,500. Same monthly spend.

Here is where the math splits. Shared leads typically close at around 10 percent in my experience, a realistic range given that the same lead goes to three to five contractors at once. Twenty leads at 10% is 2 jobs a month. At $4,800 gross profit per roof ($12,000 job at 40% margin), that is $9,600 in profit against $1,500 in spend. Fine, but flat. Month 24 looks exactly like month 1.

SEO leads are exclusive. You are the only roofer getting the call. Exclusive leads typically close at 20% to 30% (typical market range; no race against four other contractors). Even at a modest ramp, by month 8 to 12 a working campaign can generate 10 to 15 exclusive leads a month (illustrative scenario). At 25% close, that is 3 jobs. Same $4,800 profit per roof. That is $14,400 in profit against the same $1,500 spend, and the curve is still climbing.

Most roofers run both during the ramp: you keep buying leads to keep the trucks moving, and you treat the SEO spend as the investment that eventually replaces that dependency. The math still works; it just takes an honest 12-month view.

The catch? Months 1 through 6 favor buying leads. Your site is new, rankings take time, and the phone is quiet. You are spending money and waiting. But from month 9 on, the SEO column keeps growing while the lead-buying column stays flat forever.

Chapter 05

What actually moves the needle for roofers

Not all SEO work is equal. For a roofing company, a short list of things drives almost all the results.

Your Google Business Profile is the single highest-leverage asset. Most roofing searches end in the map pack. A complete profile with real photos and steady reviews will outperform a perfect website with a neglected profile every time.

Reviews are the engine. In most markets I have looked at, roofers with 100 or more reviews tend to dominate the map pack. That is a pattern I see consistently, not a published study, but it holds enough that I treat it as a floor, not a ceiling. Google trusts businesses that customers vouch for, and so do homeowners. If your SEO provider is not pushing you hard on reviews, they are missing the biggest lever.

Service plus city pages matter. A page for "roof replacement in [your city]" gives Google something specific to rank. Generic "we do roofing" pages do not. Each core service in each city you serve deserves its own page.

Local authority links move the map pack. A handful of real links from real local sites each month compounds into map pack movement. This is slow, unglamorous work. It is also what separates campaigns that work from campaigns that stall.

Chapter 06

Red flags: how to tell your SEO is going nowhere

Most SEO failures are not dramatic. They are slow. Money leaves your account every month, reports arrive, and nothing changes. Here is how to spot it early.

Red flag one: the report is all they deliver. Rankings screenshots, traffic charts, keyword lists. Ask them what was published or built this month. If they cannot point to a specific page, link, or improvement, you are paying for monitoring, not marketing.

Red flag two: no review strategy. If your provider has never asked you about reviews, they do not understand local SEO for roofers. Walk away.

Red flag three: they guaranteed rankings. No one can guarantee Google rankings. Not in 30 days, not ever. A guarantee is a confession that they will say anything to close you.

Red flag four: traffic is up, calls are flat. Your traffic chart climbs. Your phone does not ring. That usually means they are ranking you for keywords with no buying intent.

Red flag five: they are hard to reach. If they are slow to answer when you are paying them, imagine how hard they are working when you are not watching.

Demand a monthly accounting: what was built, what moved, what is next. Three things, in plain English. Any provider doing real work answers in five minutes.

Chapter 07

How to tell if SEO will work for YOUR company

SEO does not work equally for every roofer. Three things determine whether it is worth it for you.

Your market. In a town with 15 roofers, SEO can dominate fast. In a metro with 400 roofers, it takes longer and costs more. Calibrate the timeline honestly.

Your review base. If you have 8 reviews and competitors have 200, start asking every happy customer this week, before you spend another dollar on anything else.

Your patience. If you need 10 jobs next month, SEO is the wrong tool for this month. But start the clock now anyway, because the roofer who starts today dominates the map pack next year. The roofer who started 12 months ago is already there. The question is whether you are the one who starts today or the one who wishes they had.

If all three check out, SEO is the highest-ROI channel for most roofers over two years. Not magic. It is the only channel where the asset appreciates instead of expiring.

SEO works for roofers. It works slowly, quietly, and best for owners who start before they desperately need it.

Quick answers

How long does roofing SEO take to show results?

Plan on 4 to 8 months as a working guideline before a campaign hits its stride. You may see early signals at months 3 to 4, like movement in the map pack for less competitive searches. Meaningful call volume usually arrives between months 5 and 8. Anyone promising page-one rankings in 30 days is selling you a fantasy.

How much does roofing SEO cost per month?

A real local SEO campaign for a roofing company runs $1,500 to $5,000 a month (typical market range). Below that range you are usually getting automated reports, not actual work. The range depends on market competitiveness and how aggressive the campaign is. One-time website builds are separate, typically $2,000 to $5,000 for a professional roofing site (typical market range).

Can I do roofing SEO myself?

You can handle basics: keep your Google Business Profile updated, ask every customer for a review, and build a page per service and city. But technical SEO and authority building take real expertise and consistent hours. In my experience, most owners who try to DIY this stall within a couple of months. They are running a roofing company, not a marketing agency.

Why is my SEO not generating calls?

Usually: your provider targets keywords with no buying intent, your Google Business Profile is neglected, you have too few reviews, or your site has no clear contact path. Ask for three things monthly: what was built, what moved in calls, and what is next. If they cannot answer, that is your answer.

Is SEO better than Google Ads for roofers?

Different jobs. Google Ads gets calls this week at $30 to $80+ per click in competitive markets (typical range). SEO takes months but cost per lead drops over time and you own the asset. Run both: ads for now, SEO for later.

Does roofing SEO work in small towns or rural markets?

Yes, and often faster than in big metros. Fewer competitors means less work to reach the map pack. The catch is search volume: a small town only searches for roofers so many times a month. In small markets, SEO plus a strong review base can make you the obvious choice quickly. Just calibrate expectations to the size of the pond.
