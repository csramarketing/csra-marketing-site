The real cost per roofing lead: Thumbtack vs. Angi vs. HomeAdvisor.

Thumbtack, Angi, and HomeAdvisor all sell roofing leads, but they don't charge the same, they don't work the same, and two of the three are owned by the same company.

Below, I'll lay out what each platform actually costs, how many other roofers see the same lead, and what a booked job really runs you on each one, so you can decide with real numbers instead of sales calls.

Chapter 01

Two of these three platforms are owned by the same company

Here's the deal: Angi and HomeAdvisor are both owned by Angi Inc. Same parent company, same lead marketplace underneath, different brand on the front door. (Angi Inc. is the publicly traded company, NASDAQ: ANGI, formed when IAC merged HomeAdvisor and Angie's List in 2017. You can verify it in their public company filings.)

Why does that matter? Because comparing Angi to HomeAdvisor is closer to comparing two pricing plans than two competitors. The sales reps won't frame it that way. The invoices will. And that means any pricing "competition" between them is structural, not real.

Thumbtack is the actual independent alternative. Different company, different model, different incentives. So the honest comparison is really Thumbtack versus Angi Inc.'s two storefronts.

Keep that in your head for the rest of this post. Every price and mechanic below makes more sense once you know who's actually on the other side of the table.

And keep this in the back of your mind too: every dollar you hand a platform is a dollar that could be building your own rankings. I do local SEO for roofing companies that makes the phone ring without a per-lead invoice. That is the alternative this whole post is really about.

Chapter 02

How Thumbtack charges roofers (and why it's the odd one out)

Thumbtack works like a bidding marketplace. A homeowner posts a roofing job, and you pay every time you send a quote. You only pay when you choose to pursue the lead.

That pay-per-contact model changes the psychology. You're spending money before you've spoken to anyone. Send ten quotes at $50 each and you've spent $500 with zero conversations guaranteed.

Why? Because the homeowner picks who to talk to. Your quote competes with up to four other roofers' quotes on the same job. Price, reviews, and response speed decide who gets the call, not who paid the most.

In roofing, Thumbtack leads typically run $30 to $80 each (typical market range). Storm repair and full replacements sit at the high end. Gutter cleaning and minor repairs sit at the low end.

The platform also grades your profile. Roofers with more reviews and faster response times get shown first. New profiles pay the same per quote but get seen less. That's the catch nobody mentions in the sales pitch.

Chapter 03

How Angi charges roofers (the subscription you didn't ask about)

Angi runs on a two-part model. You pay a subscription for your listing, then you pay again for each lead. Miss that second part and the math surprises you.

The subscription gets you a profile, some placement, and access to the lead flow. The leads themselves are sold separately, and roofing leads are among the priciest categories on the platform.

Roofing leads on Angi typically run $50 to $150 each (typical market range), on top of whatever your membership costs. High-ticket jobs like full replacements land at the top of that range.

Here's the deal: most Angi leads are shared with 2 to 4 other roofers (typical market range). You're paying full price for a lead that three competitors got for the same price.

Chapter 04

How HomeAdvisor charges roofers (Angi's sibling, similar playbook)

HomeAdvisor also sells roofing leads on a membership-plus-per-lead model. You pay an annual membership fee, then buy leads individually as they come in.

Sound familiar? It should. Same parent company, same marketplace DNA. The mechanics rhyme because the company is the same.

Roofing leads on HomeAdvisor typically run $25 to $100 each (typical market range), plus the membership. Exact pricing shifts by market and lead type, and the platform has run several pricing iterations, so treat any number a rep gives you as a starting bid, not a fact.

The sharing works the same way too. Expect 2 to 4 roofers on most leads (typical market range). You're buying a footrace, not a customer.

One difference worth knowing: HomeAdvisor leans harder on its contractor network branding, which means homeowners sometimes trust the platform more than the roofer. That trust doesn't transfer to you. When the job goes well, the homeowner remembers HomeAdvisor. When it goes badly, they remember your company name.

Chapter 06

The shared-lead problem nobody prices into the pitch

The table above hides the real tax. You're not buying a lead. You're buying a chance to compete for a lead.

When three roofers get the same homeowner's number, the first one to call usually wins. Not the best roofer. Not the cheapest. The fastest.

Why? Because the homeowner called a platform for convenience. Whoever answers first feels like the most professional option. The other two paid full price to leave a voicemail.

This is where the platforms' incentives split from yours. They get paid when the lead is delivered, not when you close it. Whether you win or lose the footrace, the invoice looks the same.

Speed-to-lead is the only lever you control here. The first roofer to call usually wins. Waiting an hour means two other roofers have already had the conversation. If you are buying shared leads without a rapid-response system, you are funding your competitors' wins.

Chapter 07

What a booked job really costs (the math that decides everything)

Let's finish the math honestly. I'll use the worked examples from Chapter 5 and the locked $4,800 gross profit figure.

Thumbtack at $275 per booked job (worked example): that's about 6 percent of gross profit per job. Manageable as a supplement. Painful as a strategy, because you're paying per quote whether or not the homeowner ever responds.

Angi at $500 per booked job (worked example), plus the subscription: that's over 10 percent of gross profit, every job, forever. The subscription never goes away. The lead fees never drop. There is no volume discount on your own growth.

HomeAdvisor at $312 per booked job (worked example), plus membership: roughly 7 percent of gross profit. Cheaper than Angi's storefront, same parent company, same shared-lead dynamics.

Now compare that to owned channels. In the worked example I ran in my Google Ads vs. SEO cost per lead post, an SEO-driven lead hits roughly $125 by month 24. That costs less than half of the cheapest platform lead in the same model, and it keeps getting cheaper.

The platforms aren't scams. They're expensive conveniences. The question is never "do they work." It's "what are they costing you per booked job, and what would that money buy if you owned the channel instead."

Chapter 08

My verdict: use them as a bridge, never as the foundation

Here's where I land. All three platforms can buy you jobs this month. None of them builds you anything that lasts.

If crews are idle and you need work now, Thumbtack's pay-per-quote model wastes the least money while you test. You control the spend quote by quote. That's the best of a costly bunch for short-term needs.

Set a 60-day test window before you spend a dollar. If your true cost per booked job comes out above 10 percent of your gross profit per job, stop buying and put that budget toward your own site instead. The platform does not get better with time. Your own rankings do.

If you're already deep in Angi or HomeAdvisor, track your true cost per booked job for 90 days before renewing anything. Most roofers I talk to have never done this math. The subscription renews, the leads keep coming, and nobody checks what each job actually cost.

And whatever you do, don't let a platform become your only pipeline. Every dollar that goes to a lead marketplace is a dollar not building your own website, your own reviews, your own rankings. The platforms know this. Their pricing depends on it.

The roofer who wins long-term isn't the one with the best platform subscription. It's the one whose phone rings without paying anyone for the privilege.

Frequently asked questions

Which is cheapest for roofing leads: Thumbtack, Angi, or HomeAdvisor?

On a per-lead basis, HomeAdvisor and Thumbtack typically run cheaper than Angi (typical market ranges: $25 to $100 and $30 to $80 versus $50 to $150). But per-lead price isn't the metric that matters. Track cost per booked job, which includes your close rate, and the ranking can flip. If you are not sure what close rate to expect from your own website, start with how many leads your roofing website should be generating and work the math from there.

Are Angi and HomeAdvisor really the same company?

Yes. Both are owned by Angi Inc. They operate as separate brands with separate sales teams, but the lead marketplace underneath shares the same parent company. Comparing them is closer to comparing two pricing plans than two competitors.

Why do shared leads close so poorly?

Because you're one of several roofers calling the same homeowner, and speed usually beats quality in that race. The homeowner picked a platform for convenience, so the first responder feels like the best option. Shared leads close at a fraction of the rate of exclusive inbound calls.

Can I get exclusive leads from any of these platforms?

Not reliably. All three platforms are built on shared-lead economics; exclusivity would cut their revenue per lead. Some offer premium tiers with fewer competitors, but true exclusivity (only you get the lead) is what your own website delivers, not a marketplace.

How much should I budget to test one of these platforms?

Budget at least $1,000 to $2,000 for a real test (typical market range for a meaningful sample). Anything less and you won't get enough leads to judge close rates. Track every lead to a booked job or a loss, and decide after 60 to 90 days, not after the first week.

Do these platforms work for insurance and storm work?

They can fill gaps after storms when demand spikes, but storm leads on marketplaces are the most competitive of all. Every roofer in the county is buying the same surge. Your own rankings and reviews convert storm traffic far better than a shared lead ever will.

Should I use lead platforms while building my own website and SEO?

Yes, and that's exactly the right framing. Use platforms as a bridge for short-term jobs while your owned channels compound. The mistake is treating the bridge as the destination. Set a date to re-evaluate every quarter, and shift budget toward owned channels as your cost per lead there drops.
