How homeowners use reviews

Everybody reads them. Almost nobody manages them.

1.97% of consumers read reviews for local businesses

That is basically everyone holding a phone. BrightLocal's 2026 Local Consumer Review Survey found that 97% of consumers read reviews for local businesses before choosing one.

When a homeowner searches for a roofer, your reviews are not a nice extra. They are the first impression, and the "I never read reviews" customer barely exists anymore.

2.Google is where 71% of consumers read local business reviews

Facebook and Yelp still exist, but Google is where the decision happens. BrightLocal found 71% of consumers used Google for local business reviews in 2026, far ahead of every other platform.

If your review effort is spread thin across five platforms, you are diluting the one that matters most. Put Google first and let the others be leftovers.

3.93% have bought after reading reviews, and 27% spent over $1,000 doing it

Reviews do not just move pizza orders. BrightLocal found 93% of consumers have purchased after reading reviews. 27% of them spent over $1,000, and 13% spent over $5,000.

That means homeowners are comfortable greenlighting five-figure decisions, like a roof replacement, based on what strangers wrote about a company. Your next big roof may be won or lost in a comment section you have never opened.

Star ratings and trust

The 4-star wall is real, and it is getting taller.

4.68% of consumers will only use a business rated 4 stars or higher

Read that again. A 3.8 rating does not make you look scrappy and honest. It makes you invisible to two thirds of your market.

And the bar is rising. BrightLocal found 31% of consumers now demand 4.5 stars or higher, up from 17% just a year earlier.

5.49% of consumers trust online reviews as much as a personal recommendation

Word of mouth used to be untouchable. Now half your market treats a stranger's Google review like a neighbor's advice over the fence.

That means your reviews ARE your referral program, whether you manage them or not. The only question is whether the story they tell is the one you would tell.

6.A perfect 5.0 rating can actually cost you sales

In Northwestern University research on online shoppers, purchase likelihood peaked when average ratings sat between 4.0 and 4.7 stars, then dropped as ratings approached 5.0. Shoppers read a flawless score as too good to be true.

That study measured product buyers, not roofing customers. But the psychology transfers: homeowners are suspicious of scores that look too clean.

So do not panic over one 4-star review, and never buy your way to a fake perfect score. A believable 4.6 beats a suspicious 5.0.

Review count and recency

Old reviews do not count. Small piles do not either.

7.47% of consumers will not use a business with fewer than 20 reviews

Only 9% will use a business with five or fewer reviews, according to BrightLocal. If you are sitting at 11 reviews, you are not "almost there." Half the market has already filtered you out before your phone could ring.

Twenty reviews is the floor, not the goal.

8.74% of consumers only pay attention to reviews from the last 3 months

That glowing review from 2023 is doing almost nothing for you today. BrightLocal found 74% of consumers only pay attention to recent reviews, and 32% look for reviews from the last 2 weeks. Homeowners read recency as a pulse check: still in business, still doing good work, still answering the phone.

A steady drip of fresh reviews beats a big pile of old ones, every single time.

9.83% of consumers who were asked for a review went on to leave one

Here's the good news: the fix is embarrassingly simple. BrightLocal found that 83% of consumers who were asked to leave a review in the last 12 months actually left one.

Most happy customers never review you because nobody asked at the right moment, not because they would not. The companies with 200 reviews are not luckier than you. They just ask every time.

Reviews, rankings and clicks

Reviews move rankings, not just opinions.

10.Google itself says review count and review score factor into local ranking

This is not SEO folklore. Google's own Business Profile help docs say it outright: "More reviews and positive ratings can improve your business' local ranking."

Reviews are not just reputation management. They are a ranking input Google admits to using, and one of the first levers I check when a roofer's local SEO stalls.

11.Local experts ranked high Google ratings the #1 factor in turning searchers into customers

Whitespark asked 47 local search experts to score 187 ranking factors for its 2026 Local Search Ranking Factors survey.

For pure conversions, High Numerical Google Ratings came in #1. The same ratings also rank #6 for map pack visibility.

Rankings get you seen. Ratings get you picked. If you want the full picture of whether this work pays off, I broke it down in my guide on does roofing SEO work.

12.After reading positive reviews, 54% of consumers visit the business website next

Your reviews and your website work as a tag team. BrightLocal found that visiting the website is the most common next step after a homeowner reads positive reviews about you.

Reviews earn the click. Then your site has to close the deal. A strong review profile sending traffic to a weak website still leaks jobs out the bottom.

Responding to reviews

Your replies are a public audition.

13.80% of consumers are more likely to use a business that responds to ALL of its reviews

Not just the negative ones. Not just the glowing ones. All of them. BrightLocal found 80% of consumers reward businesses that respond to every review, and 89% expect the owner to respond in the first place.

And the clock is ticking: 19% of consumers now expect a same-day response. Your replies are not really for the reviewer. They are for every future customer reading along.

14.50% of consumers say copy-paste responses make them less likely to pick that business

The catch? You cannot automate your way out of this one. BrightLocal found that half of consumers grade generic, templated replies as a reason to look elsewhere.

"Thank you for your feedback" pasted under 40 reviews reads as a robot doing chores. Two real sentences that mention the actual job beat a template every time.

Bad reviews and the money

What one star is actually worth.

15.One extra star is worth 5% to 9% more revenue for independent businesses

Harvard Business School research on independent restaurants found that a one-star increase in average rating raised revenue 5% to 9%. The effect showed up for independents, not chains.

That is a restaurant study on a different platform, so treat the percentage as directional, not a roofing benchmark. But you are the independent in your market, and stars move money.

Run the math in gross profit. Say your company does $1.5 million in revenue (illustrative example, use your real number). A 5% lift at that scale is $75,000 of revenue. At a 40% gross margin, that is $30,000 of gross profit your rating is either quietly adding or quietly taking away.

The bottom line

Your reviews are already selling. The question is what.

Your reviews are working a sales shift for you right now, tonight, while you sleep. The only choice is whether they are selling for you or against you.

Compare that with buying shared leads from a marketplace, where you rent a homeowner's attention for a few minutes and split it with three competitors. Reviews compound instead. Every finished job can add one more, and they cost you nothing but the ask.

FAQ

Google review questions, answered

How many Google reviews does a roofing company actually need?

At least 20 to stay in the game. BrightLocal found 47% of consumers will not use a business with fewer than 20 reviews, and only 9% will use a business with five or fewer. Past 20, keep going. A steady flow matters more than one big number, because 74% of consumers only pay attention to reviews from the last 3 months.

Do Google reviews really help my roofing company rank higher in Google Maps?

Yes. Google says review count and review score factor into local ranking in its own Business Profile help docs. Whitespark's 2026 Local Search Ranking Factors survey also ranks high numerical Google ratings #6 out of 187 local pack factors, and #1 for turning searchers into customers.

What star rating do I need before homeowners will call me?

Four stars is the floor. BrightLocal found 68% of consumers will only use a business rated 4 stars or higher, and 31% now require 4.5 or higher. Aim for the 4.5 to 4.8 range. Research on online shoppers found purchase likelihood peaks between 4.0 and 4.7 stars. Different buying decision, same trust psychology: a believable rating beats a suspicious perfect 5.0.

How recent do my reviews need to be, and do old reviews still count?

Fresh. BrightLocal found 74% of consumers only pay attention to reviews written in the last 3 months, and 32% look for reviews from the last 2 weeks. Old reviews still help your total count, but homeowners read recent reviews as proof you are still doing good work today.

Should I respond to every Google review, even the positive ones?

Yes. BrightLocal found 80% of consumers are more likely to use a business that responds to all of its reviews, and 89% expect the owner to respond. Keep replies personal. Half of consumers say generic copy-paste responses make them less likely to choose that business.

How fast do I need to respond to a negative review?

Within a week at the latest, and faster if you can. BrightLocal found 19% of consumers now expect a same-day response, 32% expect one by the next day, and 81% expect to hear back within a week. Speed expectations rose sharply in a single year.

Can a few bad reviews really cost me roofing jobs?

Yes. ReviewTrackers found 94% of consumers say a bad review has convinced them to avoid a business, and BrightLocal found 77% say negative reviews make them less likely to choose one. The flip side helps you: Harvard Business School research on independent restaurants found one extra star of average rating was worth 5% to 9% more revenue. Treat it as directional for roofers, but the direction is not kind to a company sitting at 3.6 stars.

Can I buy Google reviews or offer customers a discount for leaving one?

No. Buying reviews violates Google's policies, and fake or paid reviews run into the FTC's 2024 rule on reviews and testimonials, which allows fines for violations. Consumers back the crackdown: BrightLocal found 97% say businesses caught using fake reviews should be punished, and 46% say offenders should be removed from Google search results entirely.
