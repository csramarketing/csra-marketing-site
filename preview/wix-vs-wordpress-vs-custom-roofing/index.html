Chapter 01

Wix Is Built to Look Easy. For a Roofer, Easy Is the Trap.

Wix spends a fortune on ads aimed at small business owners, and the pitch works. Drag, drop, done. Your site is live by Friday for the price of a couple of pizzas a month.

Here is the deal: a Wix business plan runs a typical range of $17 to $35 per month (typical market range, check current pricing before you commit). That feels like nothing next to a $2,500 custom build. The price is real. What they do not advertise is that you can never leave without starting over. The monthly fee is not the real cost. The real cost is what the platform cannot do for you, and what it will not let you take with you if you leave.

I am not saying Wix is a scam. It is a real product that works fine for a photographer or a bakery. But roofing websites have a specific job: rank in local search, load fast on a cracked iPhone screen in a driveway, and turn a panicked homeowner into a phone call. Wix was not built for that job.

Chapter 02

3 Things Wix Cannot Give a Roofing Company

1. You do not own the site. This is the big one. With Wix, you are renting. If you cancel, the site is gone. You cannot export it, move it to another host, or hand it to a developer to improve. Your entire online presence lives on their servers under their rules. That is fine until it is not, and by the time it is not, you have two years of Google history tied to a platform you cannot leave without starting over.

2. Speed is capped. Wix sites carry the weight of the Wix platform on every page load: their editor code, their app market scripts, their infrastructure choices. You cannot strip it out. Think with Google's own mobile speed research (Think with Google, cited by name) has shown that bounce probability climbs steeply as pages get slower, and a roofer cannot afford to lose a homeowner who is standing in a driveway with a leaking roof because the page took four seconds to paint.

3. SEO control is shallow. Wix has improved its SEO tools over the years, and I will give them credit for that. But you are still working inside their box. Advanced schema, granular URL control, server-level speed work, the kind of local SEO tuning that wins the Map Pack: most of it is either limited or unavailable. For a business where the top 3 map results eat the calls, "good enough SEO" is not good enough.

Bottom line? Wix is the cheapest way to have a website. It is not the cheapest way to get jobs from a website. Those are different things.

Chapter 03

WordPress Gives You Control. It Also Gives You Homework.

WordPress powers roughly 41% of all websites as of mid-2026 (W3Techs ongoing crawl data, cited by name). It is free, open-source software, which means you own everything: the files, the database, the whole site. Move it anywhere, hand it to any developer, do whatever you want with it. That ownership alone puts it miles ahead of Wix for a serious business.

But here is what the "WordPress is free" crowd leaves out. The software is free. Running it is not. Hosting runs a typical range of $10 to $30 per month for decent shared hosting (typical market range). A good theme or page builder setup, security, backups, updates: in my experience reviewing roofing sites, the average WordPress install carries a significant plugin stack, and every plugin is something that can break, slow the site, or open a security hole.

Why?

Because plugins are the attack surface. Patchstack's State of WordPress Security 2026 report found that 91% of the 11,334 new WordPress vulnerabilities disclosed in 2025 sat in plugins, while WordPress core accounted for just 6 (Patchstack, cited by name). Every plugin is a small bet that its developer keeps it maintained. Most roofers do not have someone watching those bets.

WordPress is the right call for a roofer who has someone technical minding the store, or who pays for managed maintenance. It is the wrong call for the owner who wants to set it and forget it, because WordPress never lets you forget it for long.

Chapter 04

A Custom Site Is an Asset. The Other Two Are Subscriptions.

A custom-built site is exactly what it sounds like: designed and coded for your company, nothing extra, nothing you did not ask for. No page-builder bloat, no plugin pileup, no platform tax on every page load. It is yours in the fullest sense: the code, the hosting relationship, all of it.

The trade-off is honest and simple. It costs more upfront. A professional custom roofing site runs a typical range of $2,000 to $5,000 for a small business build (typical market range; my own builds are priced separately and never published here). And you need someone who can maintain it, though a well-built custom site needs far less maintenance than a plugin-heavy WordPress install.

What you get for that money: the fastest possible version of your site, full SEO control down to the server level, and a design that does not look like ten other roofers who picked the same Wix template. In a market where homeowners compare three tabs side by side, "does not look like everyone else" is worth real money.

Chapter 06

Answer These 3 Questions and the Choice Makes Itself

Forget feature lists. Three questions settle it for almost every roofer I talk to.

Question 1: Who fixes it when it breaks? If the answer is "nobody" or "me, eventually," cross WordPress off. An unmaintained WordPress site is a hacked WordPress site waiting to happen. Wix handles its own maintenance, and a lean custom build has far less to maintain than WordPress. But no site is fully zero-maintenance, so whoever builds it should stay in the picture.

Question 2: How competitive is your market? In a small town with three roofers, a clean Wix site might genuinely be enough for now. In a metro with forty roofers fighting for the Map Pack, you need every edge: speed, SEO control, and a design that converts. That is WordPress-done-right or custom territory. If you want to know what "a design that converts" actually looks like on a roofing site, my breakdown of what a roofing website needs to turn visitors into leads covers each element.

Question 3: Is this a test or a commitment? If you are six months into your roofing company and just need something credible while you figure out marketing, Wix is a reasonable bridge. Do not build your five-year presence on a bridge. If you are established and this site needs to produce for years, buy the asset.

Chapter 07

My Honest Verdict, No Pitch Attached

If you are a new roofer on a shoestring budget: start with Wix, but plan your exit from day one. Know what "good enough for now" costs you, and set a revenue trigger for upgrading.

If you have steady work and want the site to pull its weight: WordPress with professional setup and real maintenance, or custom. Both win. The deciding factor is whether you have someone to maintain WordPress. If you do not, custom is cheaper over three years than WordPress-plus-emergency-fixes.

If you are competing in a real market and the phone needs to ring: custom. The speed edge, the conversion edge, and the "this company looks like the obvious choice" edge compound every single month. On a $4,800 gross profit per roof (illustrative: $12,000 job at 40% margin), one extra job a quarter pays for the difference between the cheapest option and the best one several times over.

The platform is not the strategy. The strategy is: be the obvious choice when a homeowner compares tabs. Pick the platform that lets you be that, at a price your stage of business can carry.

Frequently asked questions

Can I build a roofing website on Wix myself?

Yes. That is the entire appeal. If you are comfortable with drag-and-drop tools, you can have a decent-looking Wix site live in a weekend. Just go in knowing the trade-offs in Chapter 02: you are renting, speed is capped, and SEO control is limited.

Does Wix hurt SEO for roofers?

It does not make SEO impossible, but it puts a ceiling on it. You get the basics: titles, meta descriptions, mobile-friendly pages. What you mostly cannot get is the advanced local SEO work that separates the Map Pack winners from everyone else: granular schema, server-level speed tuning, and full URL architecture control.

What is the real cost difference between Wix, WordPress, and custom over three years?

Illustrative worked example: Wix at $25/month runs about $900 over three years. WordPress with decent hosting ($20/month) plus paid maintenance ($50 to $100/month, typical market range) runs roughly $2,500 to $4,300. A custom build at a typical $2,500 to $4,000 upfront plus minimal hosting runs about the same as maintained WordPress. The sticker prices converge. The difference is what each one earns you.

Can I switch from Wix to WordPress or custom later?

You can, but you start over. Wix does not let you export your site, so migration means rebuilding from scratch and redirecting every URL. Budget for that reality: the "cheap now, upgrade later" path costs you a full rebuild plus the SEO turbulence of a migration. Sometimes that is still the right call. Just price it honestly.

Is WordPress really free?

The software is free. Running it is not. Hosting, a decent theme or builder, security, backups, and either your time or someone's maintenance fee. With DIY maintenance, figure $10 to $30 per month for hosting (typical market range). With professional maintenance on top, the realistic all-in number is $60 to $130 per month (illustrative range), plus whatever the initial build cost.

Which option loads fastest?

Custom, then a well-optimized WordPress site, then Wix, in that order as a general rule. The gap matters because Think with Google's mobile speed research (cited by name) has shown bounce probability climbs steeply as pages slow down, and roofing visitors are famously impatient.

I already have a Wix site. Should I rebuild?

Depends on whether it is costing you. If you are in a soft market and the phone rings, leave it alone. If competitors outrank you, your pages feel slow, or you are planning to invest seriously in SEO, the platform is now the bottleneck. That is the moment to move.
