How Homeowners Find Roofers: The Four Search Modes Your Marketing Has to Answer in 2027

Written by Kemar Lawrence
Kemar Lawrence, Co-Founder, CSRA Marketing
Last updated Oct. 21, 2026
One homeowner, four search modes. Most roofing marketing only answers the last one.

Ask a room of roofers where their jobs come from and most will give you the same one-word answer: referrals.

Referrals are real, and they are the best leads most roofers ever get. But they are not the whole story of how homeowners find roofers, and even a referred homeowner usually checks you on Google before picking up the phone.

Here is what is actually happening. A homeowner moves through four different search modes before anyone gets hired. Your marketing has to answer all four, because a gap in any one of them quietly hands the job to somebody else.

In this post I will walk you through each mode: the searches behind it, the asset that wins it, and the mistake that loses it. By the end you will know which of your four buckets is leaking.

Chapter 01

One homeowner, four search modes

Google has a name for this. In 2015, its Think with Google team published research on what it called micro-moments: the reflex searches people make when they need something. It sorted them into four modes: I want to know, I want to go, I want to do, and I want to buy.

Strip off the corporate language and it is simply how a homeowner behaves between "my roof might have a problem" and signing your contract.

Know Mode
"How much does a new roof cost?"

Researching. No contractor picked yet, maybe no leak yet. Could be weeks or months from a signature.

Go Mode
"Roofing company near me"

Looking for a business, close by, right now. This is where the short list gets built.

Do Mode
"How do I stop this leak?"

Trying to solve or plan a specific task, sometimes with water already coming in.

Buy Mode
"Best roofing company in town"

Comparing a shortlist of two or three names, ready to call one of them.

Here is the part that matters. The same homeowner passes through all four modes, and each mode is answered by a different asset. A blog post answers Know. Your Google Business Profile answers Go. A service page answers Do. Your reviews and your website's proof answer Buy.

Why?

Because most roofing websites are written entirely for Buy mode. Services, a phone number, a "call now" button. That site is invisible for the weeks of searching that happen before Buy mode ever starts.

The roofer who answers Know mode gets remembered. The roofer who answers Do mode gets trusted. By the time Buy mode arrives, the shortlist is already half written, and it is written from whoever showed up earlier.

Chapter 02

Know mode: "How much does a new roof cost?"

This is the homeowner whose roof is aging, or who just got a letter from their insurance company, or who noticed a stain on the ceiling and is not sure it matters yet.

They search cost questions. Material comparisons. "How long does a roof last?" "Signs you need a new roof." Nobody is getting hired today, which is exactly why most roofers ignore this mode.

The asset that answers Know mode is educational content: posts and guides that answer the question straight, in plain dollars and plain English.

And the research does not stop at articles. In BrightLocal's 2026 Local Consumer Review Survey, 97% of consumers say they read reviews for local businesses. Reviews are homework now, not just a final check.

The mistake?

Having nothing to find. If your online presence is a homepage and a contact page, Google has nothing of yours to show in Know mode. So a competitor's cost guide teaches the homeowner what a fair price looks like, and it is the competitor's name sitting next to the answer when Buy mode finally comes.

This is also where SEO quietly compounds. If you have ever wondered whether roofing SEO actually works, Know mode is the mechanism: every question you answer is a door into your company that stays open around the clock.

Chapter 03

Go mode: "Roofing company near me"

Now the leak is dripping into a bucket, or the storm took shingles off last night. The homeowner is not researching anymore. They want a short list of companies, close by, right now.

Go mode is answered by your Google Business Profile and the map pack, not by your website. The homeowner picks from profiles: your categories, your service area, your photos, and the review count and rating sitting right on the listing.

If your profile is unfinished, start with my Google Business Profile setup guide for roofers. And if you want to understand what moves one company above another in those results, I broke down how Google ranks roofers in the map pack.

The mistake?

Treating the profile like a phone book listing you set up once and forgot. Wrong hours. No services listed. Three photos, one of them a truck. Go mode is decided in about ten seconds of scanning, and a neglected profile reads like a neglected company, whether that is fair or not.

Chapter 04

Do mode: "How do I stop this leak?"

Do mode is task mode. The homeowner is trying to get something done: stop the water, understand what a replacement involves, find out how long it takes, figure out what happens next.

The asset here is service pages and straight answers. One real page for every service you sell, for every town you want work from, plus honest answers to the "how do I" questions sitting next to them.

That is why service area pages punch so far above their weight. A page built for one service in one town gives Google something exact to match at the exact moment a homeowner in that town has a task to finish.

And no, answering "how do I tarp a roof" does not cost you the job. It earns you the call when the tarp fails, because you are the company that told the truth when they were stressed.

Where it breaks down?

One generic Services page with every service crammed into a paragraph. Google cannot match a paragraph to a task. Neither can a tired homeowner reading it at 11 at night with water coming through the ceiling fan.

Chapter 05

Buy mode: "Best roofing company in town"

Buy mode is the shortlist. Two or three names, compared side by side. The searches turn into company names plus "reviews", and "best roofer" plus the town.

The asset that answers Buy mode is proof: review count, review recency, your responses to those reviews, real photos of finished roofs, guarantees written down where people can read them, and a call path with zero friction.

BrightLocal's survey puts hard edges on this. 47% of consumers will not use a business with fewer than 20 reviews.

And 80% say they are more likely to use a business that responds to all of its reviews. The proof is not decoration. It is the deciding round.

Watch how the modes connect, too. After reading positive reviews, the most common next step a consumer takes is visiting the company's website: 54% do exactly that, according to the same 2026 Local Consumer Review Survey. Your reviews send them to your site. What your site shows them decides whether the phone rings.

The mistake?

Asking for the call before you have shown the proof. A big "get a quote" button sitting above three reviews from two years ago is Buy mode played backwards.

Chapter 06

The referral shortcut still ends at Google

Now, about that one-word answer from the intro. A referral skips the line. A neighbor's recommendation drops the homeowner straight into Buy mode with your name already on the list, and that is the warmest lead in roofing.

But watch what happens next.

They Google your name. They read your reviews. They look for your website. If the reviews are thin or the website looks abandoned, the referral quietly calls the second name they were given. You never hear about it. You just lose a job you thought word of mouth had already won.

That is the real relationship here. Referrals and Google are not competitors. Google captures all four modes, including the verification step that happens after somebody hands over your name.

The mistake?

Believing "we run on referrals" is a reason to let the profile, the reviews, and the website slide. Neighborhoods turn over. Referral networks thin out. The Google checkpoint every referred homeowner passes through stays exactly where it was.

Chapter 07

Which of your four buckets is leaking?

You do not need a report to get a first read on this. Four questions, one per mode:

  • Know: If a homeowner in your town searches what a new roof costs tonight, does anything with your name on it show up?
  • Go: Is your Google Business Profile complete, current, and carrying fresh reviews?
  • Do: Do you have a real page for every service you sell, in every town you want work from?
  • Buy: If a referred homeowner checks you out right now, do your reviews and your website close the sale, or open doubts?

Most roofers I talk to can answer one or two of those with a straight face. The other buckets are where the missing jobs went.

Fixing them is not four separate projects. It is one system, and it is the whole job of local SEO for roofers: build the asset each mode needs, in the order homeowners actually move through them.

How homeowners find roofers: your questions, answered

How do homeowners find roofing contractors?

Most homeowners find roofers through a mix of referrals and Google, and Google is involved even when a referral starts the process. Along the way they move through four search modes: researching costs and options (Know), finding nearby companies (Go), figuring out a repair or a project (Do), and comparing a shortlist (Buy). The roofers who win are the ones whose marketing answers all four modes, not just the last one.

What do homeowners look for in a roofing company?

Proof they can verify fast. A solid review count with recent reviews, clear services and service areas, real photos of finished work, guarantees in writing, and an easy way to call. They are rarely hunting for the cheapest bid. They are looking for the company that feels least risky to hand a five-figure job to.

How important are reviews for a roofing company?

About as important as anything in your marketing. In BrightLocal's 2026 Local Consumer Review Survey, 68% of consumers will only use a business with four or more stars, and 47% will not use one with fewer than 20 reviews. Reviews also decide referrals: a homeowner who got your name from a neighbor still reads them before calling.

What should a roofing website include?

A page for every service you sell, pages for the towns you serve, straight answers to the cost and repair questions homeowners ask, your reviews and job photos where people can actually find them, and a phone number that is one tap away on every page. If a page does not help a homeowner in one of the four modes, it is decoration.

Are referrals better than Google leads?

A referral is the warmest lead in roofing, because the trust arrives before you do. But referrals still get verified on Google, and Google reaches the homeowners whose neighbors have never needed a roofer. The strongest companies treat them as one system: referrals feed Buy mode, and Google catches everyone the referral network misses.

How do I get more roofing leads from Google?

Answer all four search modes. Publish content for the questions homeowners ask before they are ready (Know), keep your Google Business Profile complete so you show up when they look nearby (Go), build a page for every service and every town (Do), and stack the reviews and proof that win the comparison (Buy). One strong bucket will not carry the other three.

What are Google's four moments?

They come from Think with Google's 2015 micro-moments research: I want to know, I want to go, I want to do, and I want to buy. Each one describes what the searcher is trying to accomplish in that moment, which is why each one needs a different kind of page, profile, or proof to answer it.
