The short answer

Plan on $2,000 to $5,000 for a website that looks professional, loads fast on phones, and turns visitors into calls.

That range covers a proper build: custom design for a roofing company, real copywriting, the pages you need, and the technical foundations that let Google rank it. Below that range you are usually getting a template with your logo pasted on. Above it you are paying for custom strategy and design work that most roofing companies do not need.

$0 to $300

DIY builder

Drag and drop it yourself over a weekend. Fine as a placeholder so you exist online. Rarely ranks on Google and rarely converts visitors into calls.

$800 to $2,500

Budget freelancer

A decent looking site, usually built on a template. The writing, the photos, and the search optimization are typically on you.

What actually drives the price

Two roofing websites can look similar and cost very different amounts. These five factors explain the gap.

  • Custom design vs. template. A template is fast and cheap because a thousand other businesses use the same one. Custom design costs more because it is built around how your customers buy: big phone number, proof up top, quote form one tap away.
  • Number of pages. Every town you serve deserves its own page. A five page brochure site costs less than a fifteen page site with a page for each service area, and the fifteen page site is the one Google can actually rank in those towns.
  • Copywriting. This is the invisible line between a site that looks nice and a site that sells. Professional copy written in roofer language, organized around the homeowner's worries, is real work and it is priced like it.
  • SEO foundations. Fast load times, mobile first layout, proper headings, and the behind the scenes code that tells Google what each page is about. Skip this and you have a billboard in the desert: pretty, and nobody drives past it.
  • Booking and call setup. Click to call buttons that work on phones, a quote form that actually reaches you, and call tracking so you know which calls the website produced. The plumbing that turns visits into jobs.

The rule: if a quote seems too cheap, one of these five was skipped. Ask which one before you sign anything.

4

Why cheap websites cost roofers jobs

The cheapest website is the one that looks fine and quietly sends your calls to the competitor. These are the leaks we see most.

  • Slow on phones. Most homeowners search on a phone, often standing in a driveway looking at a leak. If your site takes more than a few seconds to load, they hit back and call whoever loaded faster.
  • No click to call. A phone number buried in the footer is not a call button. Every extra tap between a homeowner and your number costs you calls.
  • Looks dated. A roof is one of the biggest checks a homeowner writes. An outdated site makes a homeowner wonder what else about the company is outdated.
  • No service area pages. If you serve six towns but your site only mentions one, Google has little reason to show you in the other five. Each town needs its own page.
  • No proof. No reviews, no photos of finished roofs, no trust signals. Homeowners compare you side by side with competitors in seconds, and proof is what they compare. Our roofer reviews guide covers the review half of this.
  • Contact info is hard to find. It sounds basic because it is. If a homeowner cannot find how to reach you in five seconds, the site has failed its one job.

5

What to ask before you hire anyone

Five questions separate the builders who sell jobs from the ones who sell templates. Ask all five before you sign.

The five question vetting list

Ask these on the first call. The answers tell you everything.

  • Show me roofing websites you have built. Not restaurants, not law firms. Roofing. If they have never built for a contractor, you are paying for their learning curve.
  • Who writes the copy? If the answer is "you do," you are about to do the hardest part of the project yourself. Professional copy is a big part of what you are paying for.
  • Is SEO built in or added later? Speed, mobile layout, and page structure have to be built in from day one. Bolting them on afterward costs more and works worse.
  • Who owns the site if we part ways? You should own your domain, your site, and your content outright. If the builder holds the keys, you are renting, not owning.
  • How fast will it load on a phone? Ask for a number, not a promise. Anything consistently slow is a leak you are paying to install.

Two more worth asking: what is the timeline, and what happens after launch when you need a change? A builder with clear answers to all seven is a builder who has done this before. Our web design for roofers page lays out how we answer each one.

6

Should you just build it yourself?

Sometimes yes. Here is the honest breakdown.

DIY makes sense whenYou need a simple placeholder so you exist online, the budget is genuinely tight, and you are comfortable with tech. A basic one page site with your number and service area beats no site at all.

Hire whenYou want calls from Google, you are competing for the map pack in your towns, or your time is better spent on roofs. Your hourly rate on a job is higher than any designer's. Every weekend spent fighting a website builder is a weekend not selling work.

The middle path most roofers take: hire the build once, done right, then handle small updates yourself. You get the professional foundation without an ongoing bill for things you can do in five minutes.
