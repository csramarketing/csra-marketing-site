Why reviews decide who gets the call

Before a homeowner in Augusta ever dials a number, they compare two things: your stars and your review count.

A roof is one of the biggest checks a homeowner writes, and most of them have never hired a roofer before. So they lean on the people who have. When Google shows three roofing companies side by side in the map pack, the one with the strongest reviews looks like the safe choice, and the safe choice gets the call.

Stars first

Homeowners scan, they do not study

Most people decide in seconds. Star rating and review count are the two numbers they can compare at a glance, so those numbers do the selling before your website ever loads.

A 4.8 rating with 120 reviews beats a perfect 5.0 with 9 reviews. Nine happy customers could be friends and family. One hundred twenty is a track record.

2

When to ask: timing is everything

The best moment to ask for a review is the moment the homeowner is happiest. That moment has an address: their driveway, right after the job is done.

The walkthrough is done. The yard is clean. The new roof looks great and the homeowner is relieved it is over. That relief is your window. Ask right then, before the crew leaves the driveway, while the good feeling is fresh and the phone is already in their hand.

Waiting even 48 hours drops response rates hard. Life moves on, the roof fades into the background, and your request becomes one more thing on a list. The same homeowner who would have happily tapped five stars on Friday afternoon will ignore the same request on Monday.

The rule: no crew leaves a driveway without asking. It is part of finishing the job, right alongside the final walkthrough and the cleanup.

  • Best moment: right after the final walkthrough, before the trucks pull out.
  • Still good: the follow up text, sent 30 minutes after the crew leaves.
  • Too late: days later, when the experience is no longer fresh and the ask feels like a chore.

That window matters even more after a storm, when crews are running from job to job and homeowners are comparing reviews fast. Our storm damage marketing guide covers how to handle that surge without letting review asks slip.

3

What the crew should say

Most crews skip the ask because they do not know how to say it without sounding pushy. Give them the words. These scripts are short, natural, and easy to say out loud.

Foreman · Full roof replacement
“We are glad we could take care of the roof for you. Would you mind leaving us a quick Google review? It takes about a minute, and it really helps a local company like ours. I can text you the link right now so you do not have to search for it.”

Say it after the walkthrough, not while packing up. Eye contact, smile, done. Then actually send the link.

Crew lead · Repair or smaller job
“Before we head out, can I ask a quick favor? If you were happy with the work today, a Google review helps the next homeowner in the neighborhood find us. I will send the link to your phone before we pull out of the driveway.”

Smaller jobs count too. A $600 repair review carries the same weight in the map pack as a full replacement review.

Office · Follow up call
“Hi, this is the office at [Company Name] calling to check on your new roof. Is everything looking good? Glad to hear it. Would you do us a favor and leave a quick Google review? I am texting you the link as we speak, so it will be right at the top of your messages.”

For jobs where the crew missed the ask. The check in comes first, the request second. Never lead with the ask.

Pick the script that fits your crew and use it every time. Consistency beats charisma. A plain ask delivered on every job will outperform a brilliant ask delivered once a month.

4

The text message template

Thirty minutes after the crew leaves, the office sends this text. The homeowner is still home, still happy, and their phone is already in their hand.

Keep it short, use their first name, and always include the direct review link. Swap the bracketed parts for your details and save it as a template so anyone in the office can send it in seconds.

Text · 30 minutes after the crew leaves

Hi [Name], this is [Company]! Glad we got your roof taken care of today. Would you leave us a quick Google review? It takes about a minute: [YOUR REVIEW LINK] Thank you!
  • One text, one link. No extra questions, no second link, nothing to figure out.
  • Send it from a number they recognize. The same number you used to schedule the job works best.
  • If they reply with thanks, reply back warmly. A short “Thank you, we appreciate you!” keeps the door open for referrals.
5

The follow up email

For customers who did not respond to the text, one short email the next morning. Some homeowners simply live in their inbox more than their messages.

Email · Next morning, non responders only

Subject:How did we do, [First Name]?
Hi [First Name], Thanks again for trusting [Company Name] with your roof. If we earned it, would you leave us a quick Google review? It takes about a minute, and it helps other homeowners in the CSRA find a roofer they can trust: [YOUR REVIEW LINK] And if anything about the job fell short, just reply to this email and we will make it right. Thanks, The [Company Name] Team

Notice the second promise in that email. Giving an unhappy customer an easy way to reach you directly means problems land in your inbox instead of on your Google profile.

6

Beating the “I will do it later” problem

“Later” is where reviews go to die. The fix is not asking harder. The fix is removing every bit of friction between the homeowner and the review form.

  • Text the direct review link. One tap should land them on the review form. Never ask a homeowner to search for your company and hunt for the review button. Most will not.
  • Ask while you are still there. The in person ask plants the intention. The text makes it easy to act on. Together they do the heavy lifting.
  • One ask in person plus one text is the system. Add the next morning email only for people who did not respond, then stop. Two or three polite touches build goodwill. A fourth one burns it.
  • Put the link where customers already look. The final invoice and the receipt email are natural homes for the same link, so it is always one tap away.

This is also why reviews are part of local SEO and not a separate chore. A steady flow of reviews, earned one finished job at a time, is one of the strongest signals your company can send.

7

Responding to reviews

Respond to every review, good and bad, within a couple of days. Your response is not really for the person who wrote it. It is for the next homeowner reading it.

A thoughtful reply to a glowing review shows you are grateful and paying attention. A calm reply to a negative review shows future customers exactly how you handle problems: like a pro. Both build trust before the phone ever rings.

★★★★★

“Crew showed up on time, replaced our roof in one day, and left the yard spotless. Easiest contractor experience we have ever had.”

Sample five star review

Your response“Thank you for the kind words, and thank you for trusting us with your home. We will pass this along to the crew. If you ever need anything roof related down the road, we are one call away.”

★★★★★

“The roof itself looks good, but the crew left nails in the driveway and we found one in a tire.”

Sample negative review

Your response“Thank you for the honest feedback, and we are sorry about the nails. That is not the cleanup standard we hold ourselves to. We would like to come back out and run a magnet sweep of the driveway and yard at no charge. Please call our office and we will get it scheduled today.”

  • Stay calm, always. Never argue in public. A defensive reply costs you more calls than the bad review ever will.
  • Take it offline fast. Acknowledge the problem, offer a concrete fix, and give them a phone number to call.
  • Thank the happy ones by name. A personal reply takes thirty seconds and turns a reviewer into a referral source.
8

How reviews move your map pack rankings

In plain English: Google trusts businesses that real customers vouch for, and it shows those businesses first.

When someone searches “roofer near me” in Grovetown or Evans, Google has to pick three companies for the map pack in about a second. It leans on signals it can verify, and reviews are near the top of that list. A company with plenty of recent, positive reviews looks active, trusted, and worth showing. A company with four reviews from two years ago looks like a gamble.

More reviews

Count is proof of volume. A company with 120 reviews has clearly done the work, and Google treats that track record as a reason to rank it higher.

Better ratings

A strong average rating tells Google your customers leave happy. You do not need perfection. A steady 4.7 or 4.8 with honest responses looks real and earns trust.

Steady new reviews

Fresh reviews signal a business that is busy right now. Ten reviews a month, every month, beats sixty reviews in one burst followed by silence.

9

The weekly habit

Review generation is not a campaign you run once. It is a habit your office runs every week, in about fifteen minutes.

Every Friday, before the week closes out

Run this list, log the numbers, and start next week clean.

  • Count the jobs finished this week. Every one of them should have gotten an in person ask before the crew left.
  • Confirm the follow up text went out for every finished job, and the morning email for anyone who did not respond.
  • Reply to every new review, good and bad. Nothing sits unanswered over the weekend.
  • Log the new totals: review count and average rating, tracked week over week so you can see the climb.
  • Share the best review of the week with the crew. The team that hears the praise keeps asking for it.

Fifteen minutes a week. That is the whole system: ask in the driveway, text the link, send one email, respond to everything, and track the count on Friday. Run it for a quarter and your map pack position will show it.
